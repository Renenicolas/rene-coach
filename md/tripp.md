1. **Who he is.** A 2026-certified **GOATA Level 3** coach (GOATA = Gary Ward's "Greatest Of All Time Actions" movement system), ex high-school football/baseball player, ex heavy lifter ("former 400 pound squatter" [81]) who **herniated two low-back discs with pain down his legs** (his "MY STORY" carousel [1]) and says doctors, PT, injections and meds didn't fix it, but a GOATA "recode" did. 7. **What to ignore:** "never lift a barbell," "every non-contact injury is inside-ankle-bone collapse," "duck feet make you a serial killer / ugly / change your palate," raw eggs and raw milk, sugar-first-protein-later for a lifter, and his anti-science posts. ## 1. His philosophy (the "why" behind everything)

**What he says**
- "Most people train for ego. Very few train for freedom" [4]. The most valuable thing in life is "complete movement freedom... with no pain" [27], above money, time and even blood-test health ("If all my blood metrics are good... but it hurts to get out of bed... is that even health?").
- **The body is a pressure system, not a pile of parts.** "Pain isn't random. It's pressure showing up somewhere" [63]. "You don't pull air into your lungs, you create a pressure drop" ... the musculoskeletal system "utilizes the load and release of pressure to move you forward" [104]. When pain shows up "there's just a leak in the pressure system", which "could be at the foot level or the hip level" [104]. His goal: "an airtight pressure system without any leaks" [104].
- **There is one correct pattern.** "There's a correct way to move" [24]; "not all food is good food... why do you think your movement is any different?" [24]. "If every training style 'worked,' there wouldn't be injuries" [106]. He models training on "the people who never got hurt" (WWII bomber survivorship diagram) and then adds "volume and intensity to those postures, those positions, those patterns" [175].
- **Where the pattern comes from:** animals, babies, tribes, great athletes (Jordan, Brady, Federer, Bolt, Griffey) and healthy old people. "All movement is a spiral" [123], shown with footprints dragged in sand making a double helix.
- **Modern life broke it:** chairs, thick cushioned shoes, and gym lifting "with crooked feet, driving through the heels, a stiff braced spine and thrusting the hips to the front" [22]. Barbell lifting started as a circus act (Eugen Sandow) [22][106].
- **Retrace your steps** ("babymaxxing"): "If you're building a piece of furniture and it's messed up... you retrace your steps" [136][147]. Go back to the floor, tummy time, rocking, crawling, then standing patterns.
- **"All of health and wellness is cope for structural dysfunction"** [149]: people chase seed oils and microplastics while "the shin is sliding off the foot, almost like a melted ice cream cone."
- **Top 3 needle movers for health:** "1) posture and movement patterns 2) circadian environment 3) metabolic restoration" [155].
- **Durability is "the major"**; strength, size and power are "the minors" [200]. "Build a good structure first, then add athleticism and strength on top of it" [194]. "You can also squat and deadlift GOATA" once the structure is right [194] (note: a softer line than his "barbells are gay" posts).
- **Effortless beats grind:** "the guys who win, they're just effortless with it" [152]; "No pain no gain is BS" [100].

**Why he says it works:** his own story (herniated discs, now "sprint pain free whenever I want" [150]) and slow-motion video of athletes. He admits "I don't make the rules I simply observe them."

**Evidence verdict**
- "Pain is influenced by how load is spread, not only by weakness": **PLAUSIBLE.** Load management and movement quality do matter, but pain is also driven by tissue sensitivity, sleep, stress and training load, and research does not show one "correct" pattern fixes pain for everyone.
- "There is one correct way to move for everyone": **UNPROVEN, probably WRONG.** Good athletes move with lots of variety; injury research finds some risky positions (for example knee caving in on landing) but not a single universal pattern.
- "Train positions from the floor up, then load them": **PLAUSIBLE and low risk.** It's a reasonable warm-up/accessory philosophy.
- "Barbells are useless / only a circus act": **WRONG.** Strength training lowers sports-injury risk in studies (large meta-analyses find roughly a third fewer injuries), and heavy lifting is safe when dosed well. See section 10.

## 2. Foot and ankle (his #1 topic)

**What he says**
- "The feet are the foundation to the structure. If you have a weak foundation, the structure is going to collapse" [3]. Feet have "almost twice as many sensory nerve endings... as your hands. You wouldn't walk around wearing mittens all day" [3].
- **Foot = half dome.** "When force goes into the central and outside part of the dome, it stays strong. When force funnels to the inside where there's no support, the structure collapses" [58]. Weight should run "along the outside edge into the ball of the foot" [58].
- **Inside ankle bone high.** "Does their inside ankle bone sit higher than the outside ankle bone? This indicates that pressure is moving properly" and predicts "healthy knees, healthy hips... get up and down from the ground well into their old age" [158]. "Structures aren't meant to collapse including your foot" [196].
- **Straight feet, never duck feet.** "Everything has to open on top of a straight and stable foot" [127][130]. His mechanism: "the navicular has to dissociate on top of the talus to open your shin and ankle up so that you can load pressure back into the posterior chain. If you don't have a straight and stable foot for that to open up on, it will not work" [111]. Duck feet = "biomechanical dysfunction and compensation" that leaves the hips stuck in external rotation [143].
- **Why people turn their feet out:** if the ankle is stiff and "can't open," the body "pre-opens" the leg by turning the foot out [180]. Losing floor sitting killed the ankle's "ball and socket" motion; "I had my arm in a cast ten years... it's gonna be pretty stiff" [141].
- **You can push off without collapsing.** "The billion dollar revelation is that you can also release into internal rotation and extension without that foot structure collapsing... I can use my toes, my foot strength, my foot musculature to resist this collapse in the arch" [192]. Textbooks pair push-off with "pronation" because they study cadavers "and a cadaver cannot resist" [192].
- **Heel away.** At push-off the heel should turn away (outward), not whip in. It is "not a cue... it's not anything special. All that is is the ability to maintain that ankle bone arch structure throughout the entire gait... the ankle bone climbs higher and higher" [192].
- **Forefoot is the accelerator, heel is the brake.** "Stop gluing your heel to the ground" [61]. "We move off the ball of the foot. Heel stays high. Pressure stays forward" [118].
- **Calf raises with heel drop are bad.** "It is not okay as an athlete for your heel to travel towards the ground" ... heel drop loads the Achilles like "all of the Achilles ruptures"; "the heel is... a kickstand on a bicycle. When it comes time to pedal, you wouldn't leave the kickstand down"; in sprinting "Land, absorb, release... There is no heel drop" [118].
- **Barefoot shoes and inserts don't fix it alone.** Years of chairs and cushioned shoes "reformed the foot"; "Simply switching shoes or putting in an insert is not going to be enough... You have to go back through a recode process" [165]. Go barefoot and touch the ground ("grounding": circulation, inflammation, mood) [3][14].
- Not the answer: "scrunching a towel with your toes" [171].

**How to do it (his cues):** stand and walk with feet pointing straight ahead and fairly narrow; weight on the outside edge and ball of the foot; big toe pressing down ("start that big toe down" [195]); keep the inner ankle bone lifted; do not let the heel drop or spin in when you land; let the knee point over or just outside the pinky toe; rest in seiza/deep squat to get ankle range (section 11).

**Evidence verdict**
- Keeping the arch/knee from caving on landing: **SOLID-ish.** Knee valgus with a flat, rolled-in foot is a known ACL risk position, and landing-control training lowers ACL injuries.
- Foot strength and balance training for bad ankles: **SOLID.** It's the core of chronic-ankle-instability rehab (balance, hop-stabilization, calf/foot strength).
- "Everyone must have straight feet": **WRONG as a universal rule.** Natural toe-out varies with hip and tibial bone shape (torsion). Forcing straight feet on a person with built-in toe-out can stress knees and hips. Aim for "foot and knee pointing the same way," not a ruler-straight foot.
- "Inside ankle bone higher than the outside one predicts aging": **UNPROVEN** (the inside ankle bone normally sits higher anyway; it's not a validated measure).
- "Calf raises cause Achilles/ACL tears": **WRONG.** Heavy slow calf raises (including heel below the step) are the best-proven treatment for Achilles tendon problems and help ankle rehab. His narrower point that you shouldn't collapse through the heel when landing and cutting is fair.
- **Rene ankle flag:** his old avulsion fracture and ligament injury make him a chronic-ankle-instability candidate. Keep calf raises (slow, full range, including single-leg) and add balance/hop work; use Tripp's ball-of-foot drills as extras, starting on flat ground. Slant boards raise the heel, which makes squats easier for stiff ankles (fine), but they don't fix dorsiflexion. Keep a direct dorsiflexion drill (knee-to-wall lunge rocks).

## 3. Gait / walking / running

**What he says**
- "The modern human takes over 150 million steps in their lifetime" [7]; "10,000 steps a day, each one" shaping you [156]. Every step "is either building your body up or slowly wearing it down" [7].
- **Force "out and back", not "down and in."** Force can "travel down and in into your knees, tendons, and ligaments... Or it can travel out and back into your hips, glutes, and hamstrings" [7].
- **The healthy pattern:** foot straight and stable, inside ankle bone high, knee opens out as you land ("a very healthy structure called a bow" [150]), "head follows the loading leg and you find this healthy bend in the spine", then "release it with this heel away motion. The kneecap turns in to release that energy" [150]. Short version: **"outward load and inward release"** [15][160] and "the kneecap loads out and releases in with the heel flicking away" [156].
- **Hip pain from walking in "reverse gear."** Without the "pull out and back" mechanism, "instead of using my glutes and hamstrings to pull myself forward 30,000 steps a day, I'm using my groin and my hip flexors to drag my legs through" [148].
- **Good gait examples he uses:** Keanu Reeves ("Nasal breather... Knees point in same direction = efficient energy flow... Straight feet, ball of the foot, narrow stance... Heel turns AWAY at release... Hips back, strong posterior chain" [35]), runway models [114], Jordan, Brady, a duck (lands straight-footed [159]). Bad: "Mouth breather, stuck in his front chain, knees point in two opposite directions, heel turns in at release, heavy heel, ankle collapsing" [35].
- **Crawling is the root of gait:** "the crawl is a fractal of the walk is a fractal of the run" [179 carousel]; crawl finisher because "the crawl is actually the earliest stage where a good clean gait cycle starts getting encoded" [199].
- Backpacks: first "wear your backpack in front" so the back works [133]; later says carrying on the back is natural, "tail back crown forward," and chairs are the real problem [167]. (He contradicts himself; the second take is the more sensible one.)

**How to do it:** walk with feet straight-ish and narrow, land softly, keep hips slightly back and chest a bit forward, push off the big toe, let the heel peel "away". Barefoot or thin shoes when possible. Crawl a few minutes a day to "groove" cross-body rhythm. Barefoot shirtless sun walks after meals [105].

**Evidence verdict**
- "Knee and foot should point the same way when you land and push off": **SOLID/PLAUSIBLE** (standard in running and ACL-prevention coaching).
- "Heel whip in = bad, heel away = good": **UNPROVEN.** Plenty of healthy runners have some heel whip.
- "A 2-degree change compounds over 150 million steps": **PLAUSIBLE idea, unproven number.** Research does not show that "correcting" normal gait prevents pain in healthy people.
- Crawling to improve gait coordination: **PLAUSIBLE, low risk.**
- Walking after meals, barefoot time on grass: **SOLID** for blood sugar (post-meal walks); barefoot for foot strength **PLAUSIBLE**; "grounding lowers inflammation" **UNPROVEN** (tiny low-quality studies).

## 4. Hips and pelvis

**What he says**
- **Back chain dominance = hip sits over the ankle.** "Every animal in the world, whether you're a tiger, an ostrich, or human being, is designed to have their ankles sit directly under their hip. This allows for proper alignment and something called backchain dominance" [161]. Chairs move "hips ... in front of their ankles" = front-chain dominance.
- **"Tail back, crown forward"** / "hips back, head forward... And the hips STAY back" [169]. "The only time your hips are forward and your head is back is when you are getting run over" [82][117].
- **Quit the thrusting.** "They squat into a hip thrust. They deadlift into a hip thrust. They kettlebell swing into a hip thrust. They hip thrust into a hip thrust... nothing athletic happens in this position with my clenched butt cheeks" [19]. Thrusting leaves "glutes stuck in the shortened position" = "pancake butt" [143][172].
- **Lengthened glutes are athletic glutes** [178]. Rocking "loads the hips in a natural lengthened position (as opposed to the chronic hip thrust)" [75]. "My glutes grew more from rocking than from squats or hip thrusts" [74] (his claim).
- **Internal rotation (IR) is the missing piece.** "The pelvis actually opens in internal rotation. If you're someone who struggles with hip or back pain, you're likely lacking that IR" [178]. "The pelvis has to internally rotate to be able to open this backspace in my hips. When I don't have access to that... low back pain, hip stiffness shows up" [180].
- **Anterior pelvic tilt is not evil.** "Demonizing anterior pelvic tilt is like demonizing bending your arm" [204]. "Your pelvis oscillates between posterior and anterior tilt when you walk and run... You have to anterior tilt to reach your hips back" [164]. What matters is "where the hips sit relative to the rib cage" [93]: APT with hips forward = bad; APT with hips back over the ankle = athletic.
- **Tight hips are overuse, not tightness:** "You don't have a tightness problem... you have a biomechanical problem" [148]. Hip flexors and groin get overused when glutes/hamstrings don't pull you forward.
- **Duck feet -> hips stuck in external rotation -> pelvis pushed forward -> weak back chain** [143].
- **Hip injuries and valgus:** says an orthopedic surgeon told him knee valgus correlates with FAI (hip impingement) [188].

**How to do it (his drills for hips):** rockers, single-leg IR rockers, pigeon rockers, spiraled hip-flexor stretch (section 8 warm-up), seiza/half seiza, hinges on slant boards "working on that internal rotation" [178], split-stance isos with hips back.

**Evidence verdict**
- Hip IR matters for hip and back comfort and for golf rotation: **SOLID-ish.** Low lead-hip IR is linked to low-back pain in golfers, and hip IR/ER mobility work is standard.
- "APT isn't a disease": **SOLID.** Pelvic tilt amount correlates poorly with back pain.
- Hip thrusts/glute bridges are harmful: **WRONG.** They are safe, effective glute builders; they just don't train an athletic stance. Keep or drop by goal, not by fear.
- "Rocking builds more glute than squats": **UNPROVEN, very unlikely** for muscle size. Rocking is a good mobility/warm-up drill, not a hypertrophy stimulus.
- Valgus-FAI link and surgeon anecdote: **UNPROVEN** as stated.

## 5. Spine, posture, core, breathing, face

**What he says**
- **Posture = how your body carries load, not where your shoulders are.** Wall posture cues make you "this super stiff up and down robot" [17]. "Is the backside of my body holding me up... Or is the front side... my hip flexors and my quads?" [17]. "70% [later 75%] of your musculature is on the backside" [17][168].
- **Posture hack:** "imagine your ideal posture as the highest point of a rainbow... go all the way into this extreme extension and all the way into this extreme flexion, the middle ground of this is going to be ideal posture" = "your longest, tallest, and most decompressed self" [183].
- **Spinal erectors are an aging marker:** thick erectors in tribes "despite not deadlifting" [158].
- **Planks and crunches compress you.** "You probably don't need to be doing planks... we're already biased to this compressed position. You need to be strengthening the backside" with "tummy time or Supermans" [44]. The core's main job (99%) is locomotion with "a long decompressed core"; only a small % is bracing for a punch [48]. "No wonder you feel so stiff" if you only brace [48].
- **Cat crawl = "toughest core exercise"**: "lengthens the front side and activates the backside"; "Anti-extension, Anti-rotation, Cross-body coordination, Spinal stability" [71].
- **Spine should bend, not freeze.** "Head and spine do not stay stiff and braced like you see in every single one of your barbell lifts" [150]. Barbell lifts "lock your spine, brace like crazy" [117].
- **Back pain fixes he used:** tummy time ("strengthened my back in the lengthened position and decompressed my spine"), rockers (hips), crawling ("spinal fluidity"); clamshells, bird dogs, dead bugs "didn't work" [36-retry]. Sleeping on a Japanese floor mattress for 60 days: "My body has never felt better" [59]; on the floor "Spine stacks better. Hips settle. Breathing gets deeper" [115].
- **Ground reset:** "hardest surface you can tolerate... lay on your back, fetal position each side, and stomach... 2 minutes each position and deep breathe... sink into the earth" = "myofascial release on steroids... realigns your rib cage and diaphragm" [190].
- **Back breathing:** morning drill "Breathe into the back of your body as opposed to into your stomach - 2 min" [5]. "Can't back breathe in the front chain" [179 carousel]; mouth breathing is "an attempt to further inflate the lungs" when alignment is lost.
- **Face and palate:** duck feet -> weaker tibialis anterior -> tibia rotates in -> knee caves -> pelvis forward -> head forward -> "SCM and digastric muscles... pull lower jaw backwards" [93]. Chairs and duck feet "narrow the palate" [112][161]; "Your face is just the receipt." Tongue "is the meeting point of all fascial lines" [179][181]. He says he changed his face "without any myofunctional work" [93].

**Evidence verdict**
- Back-extensor endurance work (supermans, prone work) for back pain: **SOLID.** Extensor endurance is protective and exercise of almost any type helps low-back pain.
- "Planks are bad": **WRONG.** Planks are fine; just don't make them your only core work. His rotational/crawl core ideas are good additions.
- Crawling/cat crawl for core: **PLAUSIBLE, low risk.**
- Floor sleeping fixes back pain: **UNPROVEN.** Studies favor medium-firm mattresses; floor sleeping is fine if you like it.
- Ground lying and slow breathing to relax: **PLAUSIBLE** (slow breathing calms the nervous system).
- Gait/posture changes the palate and face in adults: **WRONG/UNPROVEN.** Adult facial bones don't remodel like that; his before/after photos change angle, lighting and body fat.

## 6. Knees

**What he says**
- **The knee transfers force; it shouldn't absorb it.** "The knee isn't meant to absorb force... it's meant to transfer it" [113]. "What if the problem doesn't come from weak knees, but rather from loading weight and pressure primarily in the wrong place?" [49] (844 likes).
- **Stop only strengthening the patellar tendon** (knees-over-toes, step-downs). Instead, "pull back into my glutes and hamstrings to let my head travel over my foot" when getting up, and absorb walking/running force "back and out into my glutes and hamstrings" [49][113].
- **Meniscus wear = shearing.** When the ankle can't open but the hip keeps opening, the knee is caught between: "this is stable and this is opening so it's just shearing back and forth. And that's why... 15 [year olds have] these dreaded meniscuses" [141].
- **ACL mechanism:** "crooked flat foot landing where the arch collapses and the inside ankle bone drops, the knee caves into valgus, while the hip still wants to open. This push and pull at the shin bone is too much for the ligament" [170][192]. "$1M to blow out your ACL" demo: duck feet, arch collapse, hips thrust, weight down and in. "Train the opposite: straighten the foot, float the heel, open the knee, sit the hip back, then lunge from there" [134].
- **The bow:** knee opens outward on the landing, "kneecap open outside the pinky toe. That is the bow. It's simply the byproduct of stability" [201]. Not forced varus or supination: "My entire foot is down" [201]. "I have never seen an injury in this pattern" [201 caption].
- **Babies show it:** she "pulls back into her posterior chain" to stand up, "back heel releasing away" [113].

**How to do it:** in lunges, split squats and landings keep the front foot straight, big toe down, arch up, and let the knee drift out over the 4th-5th toe; hips sit back; head forward. Getting up from the floor: shift hips back and let the head travel over the foot instead of driving the knee forward. Hold these positions as isos and pulse [195].

**Evidence verdict**
- Avoiding knee cave-in (dynamic valgus) on landings and cuts: **SOLID.** ACL prevention programs (for example FIFA 11+, PEP) that train this cut ACL injuries by about half.
- "Every non-contact injury is inside ankle bone collapse. This is a fact" [195]: **WRONG.** Many non-contact ACL tears involve valgus, but also fatigue, trunk position, knee near-straight landings, rotation. Hamstring strains, for example, are not ankle collapse.
- "Don't strengthen the knee directly": **WRONG as advice.** Quad/patellar-tendon loading (step-downs, split squats, even knees-over-toes when dosed) is first-line for knee pain. Use both: strong quads AND good hip/foot control.
- Meniscus "shearing" explanation: **UNPROVEN** (his story, not research).

## 7. Shoulders and upper body

**What he says**
- **Don't pinch your shoulder blades back.** "There's no mechanical advantage to having my arms pinched back here... Human backs are supposed to look like gorilla backs... most of our lung volume is at the back of the ribs" [185]. What decides posture is the hips, not the shoulders: you can have forward shoulders and still be back-chain dominant [185][17].
- **Press standing, from the legs.** "The only time your hips are forward and your head is back is when you are getting run over" [82]. "Real in-game pressing happens with your tail back, chest and head forward, and your feet driving into the ground... running back stiff arm, a lineman punch" [82]. Cue: "think of it as if you were karate chopping somebody... use that chuck part of the hand" [82].
- **Landmine press = his bench alternative:** "sitting in the back chain... ball of foot, hips back, crown is forward... a big push through the lat"; "I'm never going to have to throw an upper body push when my feet are disconnected from the ground" [198]; for a rugby player: "open this knee a bit more, close this knee... into the corner... drive through that lat... connecting the system from toe to lat all the way even into the neck" [197].
- **Crawling trains upper body "in integration":** serratus, lats, deep core firing together, closed chain [97]. Crawl push-ups and lateral crawl push-up walks for "dynamic shoulder strength" [97].
- **Upper-body/shoulder routine for shoulder pain** [89] (kneeling, butt floating just above the heels, torso leaning forward): reverse arm circles; shoulder openers (hands behind head, elbows open/close); band pull-aparts; band arm circles; jaguar push-ups (hands and feet down, hips high, pike-to-plank push-up).
- **Climbing = pull day:** vines, trees, rock; "pulling, reaching, spiraling" [92]. Hanging/rig work shown in his gym clip ("Allowing the shoulders to internally and externally rotate") [6].
- **Throwers:** "hips stay back and rotate in that back chain position. And then the shoulders release" [176]. Misiorowski gained velocity by loading a bigger "bow" on the back leg [191].

**Evidence verdict**
- Standing/landmine pressing for athletes: **SOLID as an option.** Landmine and standing presses are good shoulder-friendly athletic presses. Bench press is also safe and builds strength; it's not "a losing posture," it just trains a different thing.
- "Don't retract scapulae in posture": **PLAUSIBLE.** Forced shoulders-back posture isn't needed; posture has weak links to pain.
- Band pull-aparts, arm circles, crawl push-ups: **SOLID/low risk** shoulder prep.
- His shoulder routine "gets you out of shoulder pain": **UNPROVEN** as a cure; fine as a warm-up.

## 19. The 20 most important lessons for Rene

1. **Keep your lifting.** Use Tripp as a warm-up, accessory and athletic layer, not a replacement. Swap only lifts that hurt (e.g., bench -> landmine press; back squat -> slant-board/heel-elevated goblet squat or rear-foot-elevated split squat; deadlift -> sled push + RDL/hinge on slant board).
2. **Knee over the 4th-5th toe, arch up, big toe down** on every lunge, split squat, landing and cut. This is the one Tripp idea with the best evidence (ACL prevention). Film yourself from the front.
3. **Do his 10-minute floor block before every session:** supine back breathing 1-2 min -> rockers 20-30 -> toes-tucked rockers 20-30 (gentle on the ankles) -> single-leg IR rockers 10-20/side -> pigeon 30-45 s/side -> crawl 2-3 min. [5][176][105]
4. **Ankles first, carefully.** Tripp says a stiff ankle makes you turn your feet out and overload knees/hips/back [180][141]. Add a direct dorsiflexion drill (knee-to-wall rocks, weighted ankle rocks) and keep slow calf raises (both legs -> single leg, full range). Don't let rocking/seiza replace real ankle rehab.
5. **Balance and hop progression for the old ankle injuries:** single-leg stance -> single-leg stance with head turns -> hop and stick -> lateral hop and stick -> Tripp-style bounds and split plyos [195][81] only when pain-free.
6. **Learn "hips back, head forward" as your athletic stance** (golf address, defense, pushing a sled). Practice it as a 20-30 s iso on a slant board or flat floor [169]; progress to one leg.
7. **Hold isometrics at the bottom of split squats (rear foot elevated) for 20-45 s** at the end of leg days [80][199]. Shaking is fine; sharp pain is not.
8. **Train hip internal rotation** (single-leg IR rockers, 90/90 switches, lead-hip IR for golf). Tripp: lack of IR -> low back and hip stiffness [178][180]; golf research agrees on lead-hip IR.
9. **Don't over-brace your spine all day;** add rotational and crawling core work (cat crawl, side bends from split stance) next to normal planks/carries [48][71][197].
10. **Strengthen the back of your body in a long position:** tummy time, supermans, unsupported rockers [44][176][36]. Easy wins for sport-related back pain.
11. **Crawl 3-5 minutes a day** or on travel days, and 10-20 min on recovery days [105][91][57]. Cheap, low-risk, good for shoulders and coordination.
12. **Rest on the floor sometimes** (sit cross-legged, half seiza, deep squat for a few minutes while on your phone) [51][109]. Use a pillow under the hips if ankles complain.
13. **Press standing sometimes:** landmine press from a split stance, pushing through the back foot and lat [198][197]. Great for golf/athletic transfer and shoulders.
14. **Stay on the ball of the foot in athletic drills,** but keep full-foot heavy lifting too. His "forefoot is the accelerator, heel is the brake" [61] is a good sprint/cut cue, not a rule for every lift.
15. **Don't force ruler-straight feet.** Aim for foot and knee pointing the same way. Some toe-out is your bone shape.
16. **Use pain as a signal, not a verdict:** Tripp's "stop doing things that hurt you" [31] is fine; standard rule = pain up to ~3/10 that settles by next morning is OK; sharper or lingering pain = change the exercise.
17. **Eat enough carbs around training** (fruit, honey, juice are fine) but **keep protein high and spread across the day** (~150-180 g). Ignore "sugar by day, protein only at night."
18. **Gelatin/collagen 10-15 g with vitamin C ~30-60 min before ankle/tendon work** is a cheap, harmless add-on [55]. Skip raw milk, raw eggs, 72-h fasts, and 8-10 g taurine.
19. **Keep the proven lifestyle bits:** morning sun, walk after meals, dim lights at night, sauna a few times a week [43][155][105].
20. **Don't pay $2,500-3,500 for this yet.** The drills are public (this report). If Rene wants eyes on his movement, a local sports physio or strength coach who films his landing/golf swing in person gives the same feedback live. Re-evaluate after 6-8 weeks of the free version.
