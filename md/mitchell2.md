## 1. His story (why he believes what he believes)
- Sabre fencer from New Jersey, Harvard, 2024 Paris Olympian (team of 4 Harvard guys, favored to medal, upset in round 1).
- **The turning point was alcohol, not a diet.** "A year before Olympic qualification... nothing was clicking... we're going cold turkey without alcohol... my next tournament I did pretty well." He calls cutting alcohol "one of the biggest needle movers." (akKcCFnZsHA; 7NWz6jhqgqU)
- July/Aug 2023: went "full animal-based" (Paul Saladino) with his high-school friends in a group chat where everyone sent their food daily. From there: supplements, sauna, cold plunge, grounding, meditation.
- Paris year: "only fruits, honey, maple syrup, grass-fed meats, wild caught fish, fermented veggies, cacao... tons of raw dairy," no alcohol for a full year, "perfect sleep full year."
- After the Olympics: "Olympic blues," started drinking again, then rebuilt. **Now: "I'm not fully animal-based anymore... much more just whole foods now... I include a lot more fiber now... tons more veggies."** Back to white rice around training.
- **Verdict:** honest story. His biggest wins (no alcohol, sleep, whole foods, accountability group) are all well supported.

## 2. Food, as he actually eats it
- **Default:** single-ingredient whole foods. Meat (prefers regenerative grass-fed and grass-finished), eggs, fish (bone-in sardines and anchovies in water are "one of the most nutrient-dense foods on the planet"), fruit, honey, maple syrup, veggies, rice, dairy if tolerated.
- **Protein first, every meal:** "prioritizing protein, you're going to be so much more satiated... build more muscle... retain more muscle."
- **Around training:** "high protein, high carb, low-fat meal that absorbs fastest pre-workout." Protein right after lifting.
- **Calories:** "I don't think it's necessary to count calories in the beginning... but counting calories, it's just thermodynamics, you will lose weight." Uses lower-calorie swaps when cutting: mashed carrots instead of mashed potatoes, Sweetgreen with triple chicken and lemon/lime instead of dressing.
- **Road food:** canned wild fish, clean jerky/meat sticks, fruit, cold brew.
- **Raw carrots:** says they "bind extra estrogen." Verdict: fiber, fine to eat; the estrogen claim is weakly supported.
- **Sardine fast (3 days of only sardines):** stunt content. Skip.
- **Verdict:** the core (protein every meal, whole foods, carbs around training, fewer ultra-processed snacks) is solid and matches your Superpower plan. Grass-fed "healthier cow" claims are partly marketing for a sponsor (Star Walker Farms).

## 3. Supplements he calls mandatory
From the podcast (akKcCFnZsHA): "vitamin D, K2, creatine, magnesium glycinate... those four." Plus L-glutamine for himself ("not mandatory").
- **Magnesium glycinate ~300 mg** at night for sleep and soreness.
- **Creatine:** at least 5 g/day. He and a friend were taking 15-25 g/day after a sleep-deprivation study. Verdict: 5 g is proven. Big doses have only early, short-term data, and you need lots of water.
- **Electrolytes:** "When you sleep, you're losing sodium, potassium, magnesium... get a clean salt-based electrolyte," with no added sugar.
- **Amino acids** only before fasted training.
- **Your overlap:** you already take creatine and magnesium glycinate. Your greens cover D3. K2 is the only gap. It's low risk but the benefit is unproven at your age with normal labs.

## 4. Training, as he actually does it
- **Lifting:** simple and hard. Morning routine session: "two leg exercises, both until failure, one or two sets each... two chest movements, two back movements, some abs." Example: squat warm-up at 135 lb, then "two pretty fat sets with 225," dips ("lean forward, hunch the chest... actually work the lower chest"), seated cable row. On the road: full-body push/pull days, elevated goblet squats.
- **Cardio:** "It's all zone two... 10-minute miles... no more than four miles." "If I had all the time in the world, I would purely focus on walking," because it won't cost muscle. Bike or walk if running makes you hungry.
- **Walking is his #1 tool:** "walking is magical... if you hit your 10k steps a day... you burn 400 to 500 calories." He paces around the kitchen island on calls and always takes the stairs.
- **Order:** sometimes fasted lifts with amino acids, but "I usually don't do them anymore." Sauna 10-20 min after lifting. **Cold plunge only 30 seconds after lifting**, since "cold plunge post-workout, not the best idea [to] stay in there for too long." Verdict: matches the evidence that long cold soaks right after lifting can blunt muscle gain.
- **Verdict:** hard sets close to failure, walking and easy cardio are all well supported. Nothing here conflicts with your split.

## 5. Light, sleep and environment
- **Morning sun every day, even when cloudy:** "indoor lights are anywhere between 50 lux to 1,000 lux... even on a cloudy day... 1,000 to 5,000... even 10,000." Then a walk ("rest and digest").
- Red light or sunrise/sunset viewing, walks after dinner, darkness at night.
- **Non-toxic stuff:** 100% cotton clothes, cold brew instead of hot coffee in plastic-lined cups (microplastics), wired headphones (EMF), ceramic and stainless-steel pans, reverse osmosis water. Verdict: the sun, walking and sleep advice is solid. Microplastics and cookware are reasonable low-cost caution. The EMF headphone worry has no good evidence.

## 6. Mindset lessons
- Accountability group chat (friends sending meals daily) is how he actually stuck with it.
- "Stack your days." Don't lie in bed on your phone.
- Olympic blues: plan what comes after a big goal.

## 7. What changed over time (don't copy the old version)
| Then (2024, Olympic year) | Now (2025-2026) |
|---|---|
| Strict animal-based, no grains, lots of raw milk | Whole foods, more fiber and veggies, white rice back |
| High-fat breakfasts | High-protein, higher-carb, low-fat meal before training |
| Fasted training | Rarely fasted |
| Zero cheat meals | "Much more open" |

## 8. The 10 things from his long-form that matter for you
1. Cutting alcohol was his single biggest performance change. Use this as your benchmark.
2. Protein at every meal, whole foods most of the time.
3. Carbs and protein before training, keep it low-fat.
4. Walk 10k+ steps daily. Pace on calls, take the stairs.
5. Morning outdoor light every day, even when cloudy.
6. Lift hard: few sets, taken close to failure.
7. Zone 2 cardio is easy and short. Walking beats running if you're trying to keep muscle.
8. Sauna after lifting is fine. Keep a post-lift cold plunge to 30 seconds, or save cold for rest days.
9. Core supplements: creatine 5 g, magnesium glycinate ~300 mg, vitamin D (+K2), salt electrolytes. You already have most of these.
10. Use a friend or group for accountability. It's how he actually stayed consistent.
