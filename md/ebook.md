# Tripp Untamed ebook: faithful summary and protocol extraction

Source: `tmp/research/tripp_ebook.txt` (full text, 2,759 lines, read end to end). The PDF's actual title is **"The Abundance Protocol"** (cover also says "The Abundance Diet", tagline "An alternative approach to healthy weight loss and muscle building"). "Lose Weight Eating More Sugar" is the lead-magnet name; the book itself never uses that phrase as its title.

Author is @TrippUntamed. He states he is **not a doctor, RD, or licensed professional**. Origin story: he was "a fat kid," and the protocol "restored" him and his sister (after "years of restriction, endless cardio, birth control, and antibiotics"). The book credits **Ray Peat, PhD** in its reference list; it is a Peat-derived ("bioenergetic") protocol with a specific twist: **daytime = sugar + gelatin only, no complete protein, no fat, no starch; one protein dinner at night.**

## Short answer (plain language)

- All day until dinner: eat only sugar things (fruit, juice, honey, white sugar, Mexican Coke, candy, sorbet) plus gelatin (gummies, marshmallows). No meat, eggs, dairy protein, protein powder, fat, or starch.
- Dinner: one real protein meal (~100g protein for men, 700-1,000 kcal). Pair protein with **either** fat **or** sugar, never both. Keep them 6 hours apart.
- Bedtime snack is "not optional" (skim milk + honey, chocolate skim milk, gelatin milk, panna cotta, cottage cheese + fruit).
- Daily raw carrot salad. Cooked white mushrooms a few times a week. Liver and oysters 1-2x/week.
- Zero "PUFA": **fish oil is on the "avoid completely" list**, alongside seed oils and "excess fatty fish."
- Morning sun 10-20 min, walk after meals, dim/red light after dark, little cardio.
- Calories: ~40 kcal/kg/day; starting macros ~60% carb / 20% protein / 20% fat.

## 1. The framework (his reasoning, as he states it)

### Scarcity vs abundance signaling
- The body "decides" to store or burn fat based on signals. Restriction, heavy cardio, and food guilt signal **scarcity/threat** -> body conserves, slows metabolism, hoards fat, brain gets less energy.
- Fueling properly signals **safety/abundance** -> stress hormones fall, body shifts to "repair mode," releases stored fat, metabolism becomes "like a fire: more fuel, bigger fire."
- Glucose is framed as "the primary fuel." Without enough, the body breaks down muscle for gluconeogenesis.

### What aggressive dieting does (he lists)
Leptin drops, T3 drops, resting energy expenditure falls, NEAT falls, cortisol rises, reproductive hormones decline = "adaptive thermogenesis." Also claims dieting is associated with depression, hormone dysfunction, and (in underweight people) mortality.

### Cardio
- Light walking good. Chronic high-volume endurance training "elevates stress hormones, suppresses anabolic hormones, mimics cellular aging."
- Cites Copenhagen City Heart Study (Schnohr 2015): strenuous joggers had mortality similar to sedentary; light joggers 2-3x/week best.
- "Endless cardio is not a fat loss strategy. It's a stress strategy."

### Traditional populations
Stayed lean without tracking. Modern problems blamed on "seed oils, chaotic macro combinations, chronic restriction, environmental toxins."

### Macro combination / Randle cycle (core mechanism #1)
- Mixing high fat + high carb + high protein is "the exact dietary pattern researchers use to induce obesity in lab animals."
- Randle cycle (1963): fatty acid oxidation inhibits glucose oxidation more than the reverse. Both at once = "metabolic friction," impaired insulin sensitivity, easier storage.
- Claims 90-98% of excess dietary fat can be stored directly; de novo lipogenesis from carbs is "rare... often under 1% of carbohydrate intake."
- Donut is bad because it is half fat and half refined carb, "not because of the sugar."

### "Why sugar is not your enemy"
Sugar "burns clean," supports T4->T3 conversion, lowers cortisol, stabilizes nervous system. Full liver glycogen = no need for cortisol-driven gluconeogenesis = lower stress hormones, better sleep, calmer mood. Fruit/honey/maple/juice without fat "behave completely differently... It doesn't store it the same way."

### Calories
He does NOT deny thermodynamics: "The problem is not that thermodynamics is false." Argues "calories out" adapts. "Fat loss is not triggered by a number on a calorie app. It is permitted by the hormonal environment."

### FGF21 (core mechanism #2, the rationale for no daytime protein)
- FGF21 is a liver hormone that rises with **low protein + high carbohydrate**.
- Claimed effects: raises energy expenditure, favors fat use while sparing lean mass, modulates stress (better sleep, less water retention, calmer), improves insulin sensitivity.
- "FGF21 rises in response to the daytime phase... Your sugar phase is pharmacological."
- Gelatin is allowed because it is incomplete (low tryptophan, high glycine), so it "does not blunt the FGF21 response the way muscle meats do."

### Cortisol
Fasting, restriction, cardio, low-carb all spike cortisol. Chronic cortisol -> central fat, thyroid suppression, water retention, poor sleep, muscle breakdown, insulin resistance. Cortisol raises aldosterone -> sodium and water retention -> "losing fat but looks softer." Sugar refilling liver glycogen lowers cortisol.

### Thyroid
T3 is "your real metabolic thermostat." Dieting suppresses T3. Protocol protects it by (1) carbs supporting hepatic T4->T3 conversion and (2) gelatin's glycine/proline lowering inflammation and stress hormones.

### Lipolysis is not fat loss
Released fatty acids must be oxidized or they get re-esterified and stored again. "Oxidation is the bottleneck," governed by thyroid, cortisol, mitochondria. Daytime sugar phase supposedly restores oxidation capacity.

### Muscle
"Building muscle is a biological luxury." Needs glucose, thyroid, low stress. One complete-protein dinner "supports muscle protein synthesis without the chronic amino acid overload that can blunt the FGF21 response." Claims body recomposition while scale stays similar.

## 2. The protocol: every rule

### Daily structure
| Window | What | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Wake -> dinner | **Phase 1: Sugar + Gelatin** | Sugars and gelatin only. **No complete protein, no starch, no added fat.** "Eat liberally. Don't nibble fearfully." |
| Dinner | **Phase 2: Protein Dinner** | One full protein meal. Protein + fat **OR** protein + sugar. Never both. "Never both in the same meal or within a 6-hour window." |
| Before bed | **Bedtime snack / "nightcap"** | "This Is Not Optional." Warm, simple. |

### Phase 1 allowed foods (daytime)
- **Simple sugars:** white sugar, honey, maple syrup, fresh fruit, fresh fruit juice, coconut water, real-sugar sodas (Mexican Coke), dried fruit, canned fruit without additives.
- **Gelatin/collagen:** homemade gummies, real-sugar marshmallows, fruit jelly with grass-fed gelatin, gelatin/collagen stirred into juice, tea, or **skim milk**.
- **Natural candies:** fruit leather, real-sugar hard candy, candied citrus peel, candied ginger, sugared cranberries, cotton candy.
- **Drinks:** chamomile, hibiscus, **black coffee with sugar**, real-sugar sodas. **No protein powders, no creamers, no nut butters.**
- **Extras:** salt, lemon/lime, ginger, cinnamon, cayenne.
- Also listed in the meal plan: fruit with tajin and hot honey, iced coffee with sugar, fruit crisps, sorbet, nice cream, frozen fruit.
- **Milk in daytime:** "Ideally no." If used, minimal and skim.
- **Coffee:** yes, but never black and fasted ("can elevate stress hormones"). Always with sugar/honey/maple or marshmallows.

### Phase 2 dinner
- Size: **500-1,000 kcal.** Men 700-1,000 kcal, ~100g protein. Women ~500 kcal, ~50g protein.
- Math: 100g protein ~400 kcal, leaving 300-600 kcal from fat OR sugar, not both.
- **Option A: Protein + Fat (no sugar/dessert).** Grass-fed ribeye with zucchini and mushrooms; lamb shank with red peppers; cod in butter; braised oxtail/short ribs with leeks; ground beef + olive oil + squash. Optional protein-only bedtime snack: cottage cheese, unsweetened milk, plain yogurt, or warm gelatin cacao milk.
- **Option B: Lean Protein + Sugar (low fat).** Lean ground beef + zucchini then chocolate skim milk; cod + red pepper then panna cotta; beef liver + zucchini then fruit + honey gelatin; shrimp in bone-broth gravy then strawberry sorbet.
- **Selection rule:** "Let the protein decide." Lean (cod, lean ground beef, shrimp, liver) -> may add sugar. Fatty (ribeye, lamb shank, oxtail, short ribs) -> skip dessert.
- Option A is "best for simplicity and strong satiety"; Option B is "best for people who want dessert, struggle with sleep, or want lower fat intake." Lean + sugar "often accelerates results."
- Ribeye recipe note: "No sugar within 6 hours."

### Bedtime snack options
Warm skim milk + honey; cottage cheese + fruit; chocolate skim milk; gelatin milk with sugar and vanilla; panna cotta; fruit with marshmallows; honey Greek yogurt; sugared fruit; gelatin gummies. Rationale: stabilizes overnight blood sugar -> avoids "3am cortisol spikes," deeper sleep, better progesterone/testosterone.

### Quantity
- **~40 kcal/kg bodyweight/day** "loose reference." (For Rene at ~91 kg = ~3,630 kcal/day.)
- **Starting ratio: ~60% carbohydrate, 20% protein, 20% fat.**
- Under-eating signs: cold, worse sleep, more anxiety. Over-eating signs: fat loss stalls, no composition change.
- First stall lever: **reduce fat at dinner.**
- No calorie counting required, "but pretending calories don't exist is naive."

### Starch
Not forbidden, not the foundation. Rice, potatoes, oats "not central." Small starch (rice cake, a couple of dates) if hypoglycemia-prone early. Vegetable guide lists white potatoes, sweet potatoes, parsnips, beets, squash, legumes, corn under "Avoid or Minimize."

## 3. Food guides (complete)

### Vegetables
- **Daily "detox":** raw carrot salad (daily, between meals); well-cooked white button mushrooms (several times/week).
- **Metabolism-safe, well-cooked, protein phase only:** zucchini (in coconut oil), red bell peppers, tomatoes (cooked; limit raw), peeled cucumber, cooked celery, very well-cooked leeks and onions, shiitake/porcini/cremini/oyster mushrooms.
- **Avoid/minimize:** white potatoes, sweet potatoes, parsnips, beets, turnips, butternut and acorn squash, lentils, chickpeas, peas, beans, corn, nuts and seeds (except occasional macadamia in fat meals).
- Rules: cook vegetables thoroughly; low-starch high-water; use salt and acid; **no raw salads except carrot salad.**

### Fruit
- **Tier 1, eat freely:** oranges, mandarins, tangerines, satsumas, pineapple, papaya, ripe mango, honeydew, cantaloupe, watermelon, ripe peaches, apricots, ripe plums, cherries, grapes, sweet OJ, pomegranate juice.
- **Tier 2, supportive:** blueberries, strawberries, raspberries, blackberries, apples (best cooked), pomegranate, kiwi, figs, dates, raisins/sultanas (unsulphured, small).
- **Tier 3, occasional:** pears, ripe bananas, persimmons, cold-weather apples, unripe fruit, guava.
- Best forms: ripe, peeled or juiced, cooked/stewed, canned in juice, dried without preservatives, sorbets/gummies.

### Protein
- **Top tier, daily/frequent:** bone broth, gelatin and collagen powder, ox/beef cheek, beef shank, oxtail, short ribs, lamb shank/neck, calf liver (1-2x/wk), beef heart, tongue, 90-95% lean ground beef, steak (sirloin, filet, ribeye, rump).
- **Moderate, 2-3x/week:** eggs (pasture-raised), white fish (cod, haddock, sole), shellfish (shrimp, scallops), low-fat Greek yogurt, cottage cheese.
- **Weekly organs:** liver, oysters.
- **Avoid/minimize:** fatty pork, bacon, pork ribs, fatty chicken (skin, wings, thighs), commercial poultry, processed meats, **whey protein isolate.**

### Fats
- **Supportive:** butter, coconut oil, grass-fed tallow, ghee, cacao butter, full-fat dairy, pasture egg yolks, wild lard, wild ruminant fat.
- **Moderate:** olive oil, macadamia oil/nuts, avocado, red palm oil, bone marrow, conventional eggs.
- **Avoid completely (high PUFA):** **fish oil**, canola/rapeseed, hempseed, cottonseed, sesame, peanut, corn, sunflower, soybean, flax, walnut, safflower, chia oil, "vegetable oil," refined avocado oil, **excess fatty fish**, conventional chicken and pork fat.
- Claim: PUFAs "accumulate in tissues, promote oxidative damage, suppress thyroid function, and destabilize hormones... no flexibility, eliminate them."

## 4. Supplements (exactly what the book says)

**The book gives NO doses for any supplement.** It says "Start low. Be intentional. These are supportive; the diet does the heavy lifting."
- **Thiamine (B1):** supports pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH), "one of the most important cofactors for carbohydrate metabolism."
- **Niacinamide (B3):** supports NAD+ pools, stabilizes energy production.
- **Magnesium:** ATP stability, insulin function.
- **B-complex:** general energy metabolism, "especially if supplementing high-dose B1."
- **Potassium:** "crucial for preventing blood sugar instability and supporting glycogen storage." Food sources: fruit, OJ, coconut water. FAQ also suggests "a small amount of cream of tartar" if shaky.

Not mentioned at all in the book (common in the wider Peat world, likely covered only on calls if at all): aspirin, progesterone/pregnenolone, thyroid glandular/Cynomel, vitamin E, vitamin K2, vitamin D, caffeine as a supplement, activated charcoal, cascara, methylene blue, red-light devices.

### Food-as-supplement "tools"
- **Raw carrot salad daily:** 2 large carrots shaved into ribbons, 1 tbsp warm coconut oil (or olive oil), 1-2 tsp apple cider vinegar, salt, pepper. Optional: 1 tbsp raisins, half grated sweet apple, 1/2 tsp sugar. Let sit 5-10 min, eat once daily between meals. Claims: "binds endotoxins," supports regularity, "clears excess estrogen through bile binding."
- **White button mushrooms, boiled at least 45 min**, olive oil or vinegar and salt, several times a week. Claim: "anti-aromatase compounds that reduce excess estrogen."
- **Liver 1-2x/week** (calf or beef): vitamin A, B vitamins, copper, zinc.
- **Oysters 1-2x/week:** zinc, selenium, trace minerals for thyroid.
- **Bone broth / oxtail broth:** "Drink this daily as mineral insurance. A warm body burns fat faster."
- **Gelatin in dinner sauces:** "Gelatin balances muscle meat."

## 5. Lifestyle protocols
- **Morning sunlight 10-20 min** to set circadian rhythm (thyroid, cortisol timing, metabolism).
- **Light walk after meals:** "one of the highest-leverage habits in this entire protocol."
- **Avoid bright light after dark;** warm/red screen filters in the evening.
- **Other nervous-system anchors:** warm baths, time outside, social connection, joyful music, less evening screen time.
- **Exercise:** light movement and walking; avoid chronic high-volume cardio; "prioritize sleep, light movement, enjoyable activity, and recovery. You cannot out-grind a stressed metabolism." Lifting is not given a program; it is acknowledged as fine when fed.
- **Alcohol:** optional; cleaner spirits, avoid beer, with food, no binging.
- **Temperature/pulse tracking:** **the book does NOT give a formal body-temperature or pulse protocol** (no 98.6 F target, no 85 bpm target, no morning-armpit-temp routine). It only uses warmth qualitatively: "You're warmer. Cold hands and feet improve... kick the blankets off at night. Body temperature rising is one of the clearest indirect markers." Cold extremities = "metabolic suppression." That formal tracking is a staple of the broader Peat community and may be part of his paid calls, but it is not in this ebook.

## 6. What to expect (his timeline)
- **Week 1:** calmer nervous system, less food anxiety, better morning energy, less bloating, clearer thinking, better performance. Scale may go **up** slightly (glycogen and water).
- **Weeks 2-3:** "visible fat loss": leaner face, tighter waist, less puffiness, fewer cravings, scale drops.
- **Week 4+:** deep sleep, real morning hunger, "continued fat loss while eating more calories than before," leaner physique, better hormones.
- Waist beats scale short term. "Losing fat but look softer" explained as cortisol/aldosterone water.

### Signs it is working
Stable energy; warmer hands/feet; calm nervous system; better digestion; regulated appetite; better sleep.

### Signs it is not working
Cold and sluggish all day; starving at dinner; coffee makes you jittery; steadily gaining fat; mixing fat and sugar at dinner; overeating fat or protein at night. "When it doesn't work, it's almost always a structure error. Not the framework itself." (Note: this makes the protocol unfalsifiable from inside.)

### Troubleshooting order if no weight loss
1. Combining sugar and fat at dinner (biggest mistake).
2. Dinner portions too large (usually too much fat).
3. Overall fat too high: cut dinner fat first.
4. Under-eating sugar during the day (leads to night overeating).
5. Inconsistency.
6. Over-exercising.

### FAQ answers not covered above
- Lightheaded/shaky: eat more sugar more often; potassium (OJ, coconut water, cream of tartar).
- Type 2 diabetes: "you can still apply this framework," introduce carbs gradually, medical supervision.
- Muscle: claims it builds muscle and loses fat.

## 7. The "metabolic reset" as an actionable protocol (what the $1,500 call likely walks through)

This is my reconstruction from the book's own steps (Part Six "Getting Started" + Part Eight "Living This Way"). He calls it "a metabolic reset," "not meant to be a forever plan."

**Step 1: Learn the rhythm.** Sugar + gelatin wake->dinner; protein dinner (fat OR sugar); bedtime snack.
**Step 2: Stock the kitchen.** Fruit, juice, honey, maple, white sugar, Mexican Coke, coconut water, grass-fed gelatin, herbal tea, coffee; lean and fatty ruminant cuts, liver, shrimp, cod, eggs, bone broth; skim milk, low-fat Greek yogurt, cottage cheese; butter, coconut oil, tallow, ghee; carrots, zucchini, mushrooms, red peppers, onions, leeks, celery; ACV, coconut aminos, cacao, vanilla, arrowroot, spices. Throw out anything with seed oils.
**Step 3: Pick dinner type nightly** (lean+sugar or fatty+veg).
**Step 4: Never skip the nightcap.**
**Step 5: Be consistent.**
**Daily add-ons:** carrot salad; bone broth; 10-20 min morning sun; walk after meals; dim lights at night.
**Weekly add-ons:** liver 1-2x, oysters 1-2x, boiled mushrooms several times.
**Optional supplements:** B1, B3 (niacinamide), magnesium, B-complex, potassium (no doses).
**Track:** warmth of hands/feet, sleep, mood/anxiety, digestion, waist (preferred over scale).
**Exit criteria ("regulated"):** warm consistently, sleeping deeply, anxiety down, minimal bloating, at or near target weight.
**Transition off:**
- Keep "sugar by day, protein at night" skeleton indefinitely if desired.
- Reintroduce daytime protein gradually (milk in coffee, Greek yogurt with fruit, collagen).
- Add starch at dinner once lean (white rice, sourdough, potatoes), not with big fat loads at first.
- Keep fruit, honey, juice, gelatin permanently as "tools."
- Cycle back to the strict protocol after travel, holidays, illness, stress spikes, or to "lean out gently."

## 10. Internal inconsistencies worth noting
- "No fat during the day" but coconut oil in carrot salad eaten "between meals" daily.
- "No complete protein during the day" but gelatin in skim milk is allowed (skim milk is complete protein).
- Spicy baked cod is labeled Protein + Sugar but uses butter.
- No-cook "protein + fat" list includes rotisserie chicken with skin and smoked salmon, both of which his own guides put on avoid/minimize lists (fatty chicken, "excess fatty fish").
- Claims "fat loss while eating more calories than before," while also admitting "even here, calories matter" and prescribing ~40 kcal/kg.
- Says eat protein "before sugar at night" but also that a 6-hour gap is required between fat and sugar.
