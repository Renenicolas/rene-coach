# Bioenergetic / "pro-metabolic" diet: what they recommend vs what the evidence says

Scope: Ray Peat-inspired "bioenergetic" eating (as sold by Tripp Untamed's "Abundance Protocol" and the wider Peat community), plus Mitchell Saron (Olympic sabre fencer, Kaya Skool). Each claim graded against human evidence. Then reconciled against Rene's current Superpower plan.

Grades used:
- **SOLID** = backed by good human evidence (RCTs, meta-analyses, position stands).
- **PLAUSIBLE / UNPROVEN** = real mechanism or small studies, but not shown to matter the way it is sold.
- **WRONG or RISKY** = contradicted by good human evidence, or could cause harm.

## Short answer (plain language)

1. The movement gets a few big things right: **eat enough, don't crash-diet, carbs are good for athletes, avoid ultra-processed junk, get morning light, sleep well, walk after meals.** Rene's plan already does all of these.
2. The "science" parts (FGF21, Randle cycle, "sugar doesn't make you fat", body temperature = thyroid) are **real words attached to overstated or wrong conclusions.**
3. Tripp's specific structure (no complete protein until dinner, ~100 g protein in one meal) **directly fights a lifter's #1 lever: enough daily protein.** For Rene it would likely drop complete protein from ~200 g to roughly 110-140 g.
4. The fish-oil ban is not supported. Rene's 1 softgel is low-dose, low-risk, and mildly useful. Keep it or swap it for 2 salmon meals a week; either is fine.
5. Best move: **keep the Superpower plan.** Borrow the harmless, pleasant parts (fruit/OJ/honey as some of the carbs, a dairy or gelatin dessert at night, no seed-oil junk food). Skip the daytime protein ban, the 40 kcal/kg target, and temperature-chasing.

## 1. What the movement recommends

### Ray Peat / "bioenergetic" core (community consensus)
- Sugar from ripe fruit, orange juice, honey, milk, and white sugar (e.g. in coffee) as main carbs; limit starch, grains, beans. Sources: https://wikipeatia.org/wiki/Ray_Peat_diet ; https://prometabolic.io/blog/who-is-ray-peat ; https://www.centralathlete.com/blog/the-sugar-diet-ray-peats-controversial-approach-to-nutrition
- Classic "two quarts of milk and a quart of orange juice" daily baseline (Peat quote on WikiPeatia): https://wikipeatia.org/wiki/Diet
- Dairy as the ideal protein; gelatin/bone broth to "balance" muscle meat; shellfish, oysters, liver.
- **Avoid PUFA**: seed oils, and often fish oil and fatty fish too; replace with butter, coconut oil, tallow. Claim: PUFA suppress thyroid and need "4-6 months to clear" from membranes (prometabolic.io above).
- Avoid raw cruciferous veg (goitrogens), protein powders, and **all fasting/restriction**.
- Raw carrot salad daily ("binds endotoxin, lowers estrogen").
- Body temperature and pulse as the gauge of thyroid/metabolic rate (Broda Barnes lineage).
- In the wider community (NOT in Tripp's ebook): aspirin, progesterone/pregnenolone, thyroid hormone, vitamin E/K, cascara, activated charcoal. These are self-medication and should not be done without a doctor.
- GQ's 2026 profile describes the pattern as fructose-heavy carbs (fruit, honey), micronutrient-dense foods (oysters, shellfish, eggs, organ meats, dairy), and no PUFA: https://www.gq.com/story/ray-peat-diet-health

Stricter and more unusual than mainstream Peat: **daytime = sugar + gelatin only (no complete protein, no fat, no starch), one protein dinner (fat OR sugar, never both, 6-hour gap), mandatory bedtime dairy/sugar snack, ~40 kcal/kg, ~60/20/20 carb/protein/fat, fish oil banned.** Mainstream Peat content actually says the opposite on one point: pair fruit/juice *with* protein and fat for stable blood sugar (e.g. OJ with cheese or eggs): https://www.ivyherbal.com/articles/ray-peat-diet-approved-foods-list ; WikiPeatia's OJ page says "pair with protein and salt": https://wikipeatia.org/wiki/Orange_juice

## 2. Claim-by-claim evidence check

### A. "Eat enough; aggressive dieting backfires" -> **SOLID (with limits)**
- Real: low energy availability and low carbohydrate lower T3, raise cortisol, and harm hormones/performance in athletes (REDs literature). Hackney 2025 review: low-carb (even ~30% of energy for 3 days) plus hard training raised basal and exercise cortisol; low energy availability + low carb drives a "low T3" state. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40639795/ ; https://journals.humankinetics.com/view/journals/ijspp/21/3/article-p500.xml
- Limit: adaptive thermogenesis is real but modest; it does not stop a sensible deficit from working. Tripp's own references (Leibel, Rosenbaum, Trexler) say adaptation exists, not that calories stop mattering.
- Relevance to Rene: he is on a recomp (small or no deficit), not a crash diet, so this problem does not apply to him.

### B. "Carbs/sugar are good fuel for athletes" -> **SOLID for adequate carbs; RISKY framing for 'unlimited sugar'**
- Strength athletes typically eat ~3-8 g/kg carbs; research doesn't support going very low. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8878406 ; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-nutrition/article/carbohydrate-intake-and-resistancebased-exercise-are-current-recommendations-reflective-of-actual-need/45C97BD7A99949777E3474E50709A7DA
- Rene is at 305 g = ~3.4 g/kg on lift days. That is adequate for 60-75 min lifting. Tripp's ~545 g (6 g/kg) is endurance-athlete territory.
- Whole fruit: linked to *lower* type 2 diabetes risk; **fruit juice linked to higher risk**, and swapping juice for whole fruit lowered risk (Muraki, BMJ 2013, 3 cohorts). https://www.bmj.com/content/347/bmj.f5001
- Juice and weight: 1 serving/day of 100% juice linked to BMI gain in kids; in adults the weight gain appears to be via extra calories (Nguyen, JAMA Pediatrics 2024). https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapediatrics/fullarticle/2813987
- WHO: keep **free sugars** (added sugar, honey, syrups, juice) under 10% of energy, ideally under 5%, mainly for weight gain and **dental caries**; sugars in whole fruit and milk are not included. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK285538/ ; https://www.who.int/news/item/04-03-2015-who-calls-on-countries-to-reduce-sugars-intake-among-adults-and-children
- Tripp's daytime menu (Mexican Coke, cotton candy, hard candy, OJ, honey, marshmallows) is mostly **free sugar**, sipped/grazed all day: the worst pattern for teeth.
- Fructose in surplus increases liver fat and DNL more than glucose (Stanhope 2009 RCT; Geidl-Flueck 2021 J Hepatol RCT; Framingham 2022 cohort, which also summarizes the Maersk 2012 RCT where 1 L/day sucrose soda raised liver fat vs milk, diet soda, or water). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19381015 ; https://www.journal-of-hepatology.eu/article/S0168-8278%2821%2900161-6/fulltext ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9236136
- Verdict: fruit and some juice/honey around training are fine for a lean lifter who is not in surplus. "Eat sugar liberally, don't nibble fearfully" is not supported.

### C. "Sugar lowers cortisol; full liver glycogen prevents 3am cortisol spikes" -> **PLAUSIBLE in athletes / UNPROVEN as sold**
- Carb availability does blunt exercise cortisol and low-carb raises it (Hackney 2025 above). That is the real kernel.
- No good evidence that healthy, fed people have meaningful "3am cortisol spikes from low blood sugar" that a bedtime honey-milk fixes. Normal cortisol rises in the early morning by circadian design.
- Bedtime protein itself is useful: 30-40 g casein pre-sleep increases overnight muscle protein synthesis (ISSN). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28642676/ So a *dairy* nightcap has a real benefit, but it is the protein, not the sugar.

### D. Randle cycle / "never mix fat and sugar" / 6-hour separation -> **Mechanism real, practical claim WRONG**
- The glucose-fatty acid cycle is real muscle/liver physiology (Hue & Taegtmeyer 2009). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2739696
- But the only RCT of "food combining" (dissociated) vs balanced diet, calorie- and macro-matched: **no extra weight or fat loss** (6.2 vs 7.5 kg; Golay 2000). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10805507
- NIH metabolic-ward work: energy balance drives fat loss; low-fat vs low-carb calorie-for-calorie differences are small (Hall 2015). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4603544/
- Kernel of truth: hyper-palatable fat+sugar ultra-processed foods make people overeat ~500 kcal/day (Hall 2019). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31105044 So "don't eat donuts/pizza/pastries" is good advice, but that is about *overeating*, not a fuel-competition trick. The "6-hour window" has no evidence at all.

### E. "Sugar is rarely turned into fat (<1%)" -> **Technically half-true, conclusion WRONG**
- True: de novo lipogenesis (sugar -> fat) is small unless carbs exceed total energy needs (Hellerstein 1999; Acheson 1988). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10365981 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3165600
- But: extra carbs **suppress fat burning**, so the fat you eat gets stored instead. "Body fat is thereby accrued, but the pathway of DNL is not traversed" (Hellerstein). AJCN editorial: excess carb energy increased body fat "not by conversion of the carbohydrate to fat. Instead, the oxidation of dietary fat was suppressed." https://ajcn.nutrition.org/article/S0002-9165%2823%2906398-0/fulltext
- Net: in a calorie surplus, sugar makes you fatter just fine. Tripp's own FAQ quietly admits "even here, calories matter."

### F. FGF21: "the sugar phase is pharmacological" -> **Mechanism real; Tripp's version WRONG for a lifter**
- FGF21 rises with **protein restriction**. In humans a 28-day low-protein diet raised it (Laeger 2014 JCI). https://www.jci.org/articles/view/74915
- Best human test (Nature Metabolism 2025, n=6-8 per arm, lean moderately active men, NOT lifters): 5 weeks at 0.8 g/kg protein needed ~19-21% more calories to hold weight, FGF21 up ~270-280%. **Same result whether protein was replaced by carbs or by fat.** T3 and resting metabolic rate did not change; the extra burn was mostly around meals. Lean mass held only because calories were raised to keep weight stable. https://www.nature.com/articles/s42255-025-01236-7 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40050437/
- Mouse work also shows low-protein **high-fat** diets raise FGF21 too, so "high sugar" is not the key; low protein is (Hill 2019). https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2211124719306333
- Fructose does acutely bump FGF21 for a few hours (Dushay 2015), but FGF21 is **2-3x higher in metabolic syndrome** (a "FGF21 resistance" state), so higher FGF21 from sugar is not automatically good. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212877814001653
- Low-protein **overfeeding** in humans led to less weight gain partly because people gained **less lean mass** (Bray 2012 JAMA, cited in the Nature Metab paper). That is exactly what a lifter does not want.
- No study tests Tripp's actual idea (normal daily protein, but none of it before dinner) for FGF21 or fat loss. FGF21 responds to protein status over days/weeks, not a daytime window.
- Verdict for Rene: the FGF21 "benefit" requires eating ~0.8 g/kg protein, which is roughly half the 1.6 g/kg that maximizes lifting gains. It's a trade he should not make.

### G. One protein meal at dinner (~100 g) -> **PLAUSIBLE for MPS per meal; WRONG on total protein**
- Good news for Tripp: 100 g protein in one sitting produces a larger and longer (>12 h) anabolic response than 25 g (Trommelen 2023). So a big dinner is not "wasted." https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38118410
- But total daily protein is what matters most: gains plateau around **1.6 g/kg/day** (upper CI ~2.2) (Morton 2018 BJSM). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/ ISSN: 1.4-2.0 g/kg, ideally spread every 3-4 h. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28642676/ Areta 2013: 4x20 g beat 2x40 g or 8x10 g over 12 h. https://physoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1113/jphysiol.2012.244897 ; Schoenfeld & Aragon 2018: ~0.4 g/kg/meal x 4 meals. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5828430
- For Rene (~91 kg): Tripp's structure gives ~100 g at dinner + maybe 15-35 g from skim-milk/cottage-cheese snacks = **~115-135 g complete protein (1.3-1.5 g/kg)**. Gelatin/collagen does not count (no tryptophan, low leucine), which Rene's plan already notes. And he lifts at 11 AM, so the post-lift meal would have **zero protein for ~8 hours**.

### H. Thyroid: "carbs support T4->T3; gelatin supports thyroid" -> **First half SOLID-ish, second UNPROVEN**
- Carb restriction and energy deficit lower T3 (classic Danforth work; REDs reviews above). Adequate carbs protect T3. Rene already eats adequate carbs.
- No evidence that *more* sugar beyond adequacy raises T3 in a fed person; the 2025 FGF21 trial found no T3 change on a 70% carb diet.
- Gelatin/glycine "supports thyroid": no human evidence found.

### I. Body temperature and pulse as a metabolism/thyroid gauge -> **WRONG as a diagnostic target**
- The American Thyroid Association found **no scientific evidence** for "Wilson's syndrome" (low temp + normal labs = hidden hypothyroidism) and called temperature-based diagnosis imprecise; T3 treatment for it is potentially dangerous. Average 8 AM oral temp in healthy adults is ~97.6 F, not 98.6. https://www.thyroid.org/american-thyroid-association-statement-on-wilsons-syndrome/
- Normal body temperature has fallen since the 1800s; ~97.3-98.2 F is normal today (Protsiv 2020 eLife). https://elifesciences.org/articles/49555
- Chasing a higher resting pulse is backwards for an athlete: low resting HR is a fitness marker. (General physiology; no specific source pulled.)
- Tripp's ebook uses warmth only qualitatively; the formal "98.6 F / 85 bpm" targets come from the wider Peat world and may be part of his paid call. Real thyroid status = TSH, free T4, free T3 on a blood test (Rene already does Superpower labs).

### J. PUFA / seed oils / fish oil -> **Mostly WRONG as stated; one kernel**
- Linoleic acid (the main seed-oil fat): higher intake/biomarker levels linked to **modestly lower** all-cause, CVD, and cancer mortality in a meta-analysis of cohorts (Li 2020). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32020162/ ; coronary heart disease meta-analysis (Farvid 2014, Circulation): https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circulationaha.114.010236 ; Johns Hopkins overview: https://publichealth.jhu.edu/2025/the-evidence-behind-seed-oils-health-effects
- Honest counterpoint: recovered Sydney Diet Heart data showed higher mortality when safflower oil replaced saturated fat in men with heart disease (Ramsden 2013 BMJ). https://www.bmj.com/content/346/bmj.e8707 Evidence is not unanimous, but the weight of it does not support "no flexibility, eliminate them."
- Kernel: most seed oil in the US diet comes inside ultra-processed/fried food, and cutting *that* is good (Hall 2019 above).
- "PUFA suppress the thyroid": I found no human trial showing fish oil or normal seed-oil intake causes hypothyroidism.
- **Fish oil specifically:**
  - Benefit in lifters: meta-analysis of 41 RCTs found moderate reductions in IL-6, TNF-a, CK, and soreness, strongest at **>=2 g/day EPA+DHA for >=6 weeks**. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41891174 One 10-week RCT in young adults at ~3.9 g/day saw bigger strength gains and fat loss vs placebo. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9970203 Muscle protein synthesis boost seen in untrained/older people (Smith 2011) but not in trained men already eating enough protein (McGlory, via review). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21501117/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11197025/
  - Risk: higher atrial fibrillation risk in RCTs of older, high-cardiovascular-risk people taking 0.84-4 g/day (IRR 1.37). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8756005 Not shown in young healthy people at ~1 g/day.
  - Rene's dose (690 EPA + 260 DHA = 950 mg) is below the dose where recovery benefits are clearest, and below where AFib signals show up in high-risk groups. So: **low benefit, low risk.** Not a poison.

### K. Gelatin, collagen, bone broth, glycine -> **PLAUSIBLE (small but real)**
- Glycine 3 g before bed improved subjective sleep and sleep latency in small RCTs (Yamadera 2007). https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1479-8425.2007.00262.x
- Collagen peptides before bed reduced sleep fragmentation in active men with sleep complaints (small RCT). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10799148
- 15 g vitamin-C-enriched gelatin 1 h before exercise doubled a collagen-synthesis marker (Shaw 2017, n=8). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27852613 Systematic review of collagen for tendons/joints/recovery: promising but small studies. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8521576/
- Rene's plan already has collagen pre-lift, glycine 3 g at night, and bone broth. This is the part of the Peat world with the best evidence, and he already does it.

### L. Raw carrot salad ("binds endotoxin, clears estrogen") -> **UNPROVEN (harmless)**
- No human trials found testing Peat's carrot salad for endotoxin or estrogen. It is a fine fiber-and-veg snack. Nothing more.

### M. Boiled white button mushrooms ("anti-aromatase, lowers estrogen") -> **UNPROVEN in humans (harmless)**
- Evidence is cell-culture and mouse work on breast-cancer cells (Chen 2006, Cancer Research). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17178902 No evidence this changes a healthy young man's estrogen.

### N. Avoid starch, legumes, raw veg, nuts; avoid whey -> **WRONG / unnecessary**
- No good human evidence that rice, potatoes, oats, lentils, or raw vegetables harm metabolism. Whole-food, high-carb, high-fiber ad-libitum diets actually lowered calorie intake by ~550-690 kcal/day vs a keto diet in the NIH ward (Hall 2021). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33479499
- Whey "avoid" is unsupported; whey is among the best-studied proteins for muscle (ISSN). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28642676/

### O. Dairy / milk -> **SOLID for pasteurized; RISKY for raw**
- Milk, kefir, yogurt, and cottage cheese are complete, leucine-rich proteins. Fine.
- Raw milk (Saron, parts of the Peat world): CDC counted 202 outbreaks, 2,645 illnesses, 228 hospitalizations (1998-2018); FDA says claimed benefits are not substantiated; H5N1 bird flu has been found in raw milk. https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/dangers-raw-milk-unpasteurized-milk-can-pose-serious-health-risk ; https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/raw-milk-questions-answers ; https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/raw-milk.html

### P. "Cardio is a stress strategy" -> **WRONG (Rene's cardio is fine)**
- Tripp's Copenhagen study had only **28 deaths among all joggers**; the strenuous-jogger hazard ratio was 1.97 with a 95% CI of 0.48-8.14 (not significant). https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2014.11.023
- Meta-analysis of 14 studies / 232k people: any running linked to 27% lower all-cause mortality, with **no significant dose-response harm** at higher volumes (Pedisic 2020 BJSM). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31685526
- Rene's 20-30 min hard cardio once a week plus Pilates is nowhere near "chronic high-volume endurance."

### Q. No fasting; coffee not on an empty stomach -> **Reasonable preference**
- Rene's plan already has no 16:8 and no 36-h fasts, and coffee comes after Meal 1. Agreement, no change needed.

### R. Light, sleep, walking after meals, screens -> **SOLID**
- Circadian misalignment worsens glucose and cardiovascular markers (Scheer 2009 PNAS, cited by Tripp). Post-meal walking helps glucose. Rene's plan already has 20-30 min outdoor morning walk, screens off 9:30, 8h15 in bed.

### S. Supplements (Tripp: B1, niacinamide, magnesium, B-complex, potassium; no doses) -> **mostly unnecessary**
- Magnesium: Rene already takes glycinate, capped at 350 mg supplemental (the adult UL for supplemental magnesium). Keep.
- B1/B3/B-complex: no evidence of benefit in someone eating varied whole food and a greens powder. Tripp gives no doses; high-dose niacinamide is not harmless. Skip unless a lab shows a deficiency.
- Potassium from fruit/OJ/coconut water: fine, food-first. Rene's plan already has pineapple, fruit, and electrolytes.

## 4. Reconciled recommendation for Rene

**Verdict: keep the Superpower plan as the base. Tripp's structure is not a fit for a 6'4" lifter on a recomp.** The two things that would actually hurt his goals are (1) cutting complete protein to one dinner meal and (2) eating ~3,600 kcal mostly as free sugar.

If he wants to try the "pro-metabolic" feel without the downsides, a safe 2-week version:
1. Keep 200 g protein across 3 meals (plus a night dairy snack if he wants).
2. Make ~50-100 g of the 305 g carbs come from fruit, OJ, or honey around training (e.g. OJ or pineapple with Meal 2), instead of more rice. Keep total carbs the same.
3. Optional night snack inside the calorie budget: Greek yogurt or cottage cheese with fruit/honey (pre-sleep protein has real evidence).
4. Keep olive oil; avoid seed-oil ultra-processed foods (already implied by his plan).
5. Keep the omega-3 softgel, or drop it only if salmon is eaten at least 2x/week.
6. Judge by his existing tracking: 7-day average weight, Sunday photos, lift progress, Whoop sleep. Not by hand warmth or body temperature.
7. Protect teeth: drink juice with meals, not sipped all day.

**On the $1,500 "metabolic optimization":** analytical call, not an identity call. The free ebook almost certainly covers the core of what that call teaches (Tripp said it is "a metabolic reset that covers nutrition, lifestyle and supplementation"). The book gives no supplement doses and no formal temperature/pulse protocol, so those are probably what the call adds. The evidence above says the core structure conflicts with Rene's lifting goals, and the parts that are solid (light, sleep, collagen/glycine, no crash dieting, no fasting) are already in his plan. It is not worth paying for the metabolic piece. The movement/posture piece is a separate question this file does not cover.
