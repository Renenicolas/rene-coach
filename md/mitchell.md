## 2. Fascia and posture work (the Mucci method, public version)

Where it comes from: his roommate/coach **Justin Mucci (@muccistrength)**, creator of the "PAP" system (mix of David Weck-style spiral/"snake" work, GOATA-style barefoot gait, Chinese-medicine ideas). Mucci coached the Harvard fencing team. The full 8-drill "Fix the Fascia" routine with doses is in research/81_KAYA_REPORT.md section D. Below is what Mitchell teaches publicly.

### 2a. How to stand and walk ("stop duck walking")
What he says (Jul 10, 2026, YouTube LqAFWu2zvd0 = IG https://www.instagram.com/reel/DanannjPNuZ/ area): "First off get rid of the shoes. Imagine if you wore mittens all day... We got to get your toes straight... Most people stand like this. Their feet are out. Their hips are forward. And their neck is in front. **Your hips need to be above your ankle. And your head should not be forward.** ... Retract the shoulders back. Keeping the hips behind you... You're moving with the back chain... we're moving in a spiral." "Your feet will automatically go straight if you focus on keeping the back actually retracted."
Also: models at the Milan Olympic opening "toes are in... they're gliding... Head is back, chin slightly down" (YouTube qck7_nsJYWQ); "Pigeon toes in. Spirals. Everything in nature is a spiral" (YouTube 4wkOX-THx_Q); IG meme "You stand wrong: hip in front of ankle -> hip on top of ankle" (Jun 8, 2026).
How to do it:
1. Barefoot as much as you can (home, grass).
2. Feet pointing straight ahead (he often says "toes slightly in").
3. Stack hips over ankles (don't push hips forward), ribs down, head back over shoulders, chin level or slightly down.
4. Shoulders pulled back and down; let the shoulders and hips counter-rotate as you walk ("spiral").
Verdict: **Plausible for posture**, **unproven** for the claims that it "shapes your jawline" or "fixes fascia." Walking with a forward head and turned-out feet is common; cueing a neutral stance is harmless. Forcing "toes in" beyond your natural foot angle is not supported by evidence.
**Ankle flag:** a forced pigeon-toed walk shifts load to the outside of the foot/ankle. With Rene's old lateral ankle injuries, aim for "straight," not "in."

### 2b. Sit on your heels (seiza) instead of chairs
What he says: "You're bending your neck watching this. Sit on heels now! And arch that back!" (64K likes) https://www.instagram.com/reel/DZWgqfEv53d/ . How: "Sit on your heels. But instead of letting them collapse... focus on getting the heels actually out. Your toes should be touching. And the outside of your heels, try to be getting them onto the floor. Keep the back arch and then just get comfortable like this." (LqAFWu2zvd0). "I hate chairs." https://www.instagram.com/p/DcEPL8Blc8f/ . Face post lists "Seiza | Lotus position" as antidotes to duck feet. https://www.instagram.com/reel/DZnFBJfPAhg/
Dose: while scrolling your phone; Kaya version = 1-2 min rockers.
Verdict: **Plausible** as a way to train ankle/knee/hip range and break up sitting; no trials on "fascia."
**ANKLE FLAG (big):** "toes together, heels out, outside of heel to the floor" = ankle pointed AND rolled in (plantarflexion + inversion). That is the position that sprains the outer ankle ligaments. Rene: use a folded towel under the ankles, keep weight on hands, 20-30 s holds, stop at any outer-ankle pain; get PT clearance.

## 13. Ankle safety notes for Rene (old left avulsion fracture, old right ligament injury, limited dorsiflexion)
1. **Seiza "heels out, toes together, outside of heel to the floor"** = ankle pointed + rolled in = the sprain position. Start with a rolled towel under the ankles, most weight on hands, 20-30 s, pain-free only.
2. **Toes-in / pigeon-toed walking** loads the outside of the ankle. Aim for straight feet.
3. **Deep squats, deep-squat calf raises, the "bow" wall hold** need dorsiflexion Rene lacks. Elevate heels (small plate or wedge) and hold onto something.
4. **Kip-ups, backflips, toe-hooked "fascial Nordics", 50-100 jumps, sprints on hard ground** = high-impact/high-force. Build up with a PT; do regular partner-held Nordics first.
5. **Barefoot training:** start on grass/soft surfaces, short sessions; old ligament injuries rely on good proprioception, which barefoot work can help, but build slowly.
