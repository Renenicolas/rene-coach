# 40 - Evidence counterweight: ankle instability, golf pain, self-assessment, athletic lifting, pain science, GOATA claim check

Prepared for: Rene (22 M, 6'4" ~200 lb, national-team golfer, multi-sport, lifts 5x/wk bodybuilding split).
Purpose: evidence-based counterweight to a GOATA-style movement plan so the final coaching plan is safe and effective.
Status: research only, no spending. Not medical advice; items flagged "see PT/MD" matter.

## 0. Short answer (plain language)

- Both ankles have a history (L: posteromedial talus avulsion fracture + severe sprain Mar 2024; R: calcification under the lateral malleolus = old ligament injury). Up to ~40% of people with a first lateral sprain still have instability symptoms at 1 year. That fits "a lot of pain when I play sports" + limited dorsiflexion. The single best-supported fix is **balance / neuromuscular training + hop-stabilization + calf/peroneal strength**, with **joint mobilization (mobilization with movement)** to open dorsiflexion. This is strong-moderate (Grade A-B) evidence in the 2021 JOSPT ankle guideline.
- The current plan's "10-minute ankle block" (band mobilization + loaded split squats) is good but **missing the parts with the best evidence: single-leg balance progressions, hop/landing training, and single-leg calf + peroneal strength**. Add them.
- Golf's biggest pain sites are low back, lead wrist, elbow, lead shoulder, lead hip. Limited lead-hip internal rotation and trunk rotation are the classic drivers. TPI screens are free to self-run; they predict swing faults, not injuries.
- Pain rule: training pain up to ~3/10 is "green", up to 5/10 is "acceptable" **only if it settles by the next morning and isn't creeping up week to week**. Anything sharp, swelling, giving-way, night pain, numbness = stop and get checked.
- GOATA's broad themes (train the foot/ankle, use the posterior chain, athletic movement, some anterior pelvic tilt is normal) fit the evidence. Its specific mechanical claims (one "correct" way to walk/squat, inside-ankle collapse causes injury, avoiding normal pronation) are **not** supported.

## 1. Chronic ankle instability (CAI) after sprains

### 1.1 Prevalence and why it matters for Rene
- Up to ~70% of people with an acute lateral ankle sprain may develop CAI; a prospective cohort found 40% CAI prevalence one year after a first-time sprain. Source: Herzog et al., "Epidemiology of Ankle Sprains and Chronic Ankle Instability", J Athl Train 2019. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6602402/
- ~40% of people who sprain get recurrent sprain + residual symptoms lasting 12+ months. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6922560/
- 2021 JOSPT CPG: CAI prevalence reported 0-73% depending on population; CAI = mechanical instability, functional instability, or both. https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2021.0302 and plain-language summary https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2021.0503
- CAI involves: weakness (esp. evertors/peroneals), persistent pain, loss of control, ligament laxity, repeated sprains, possible cartilage damage/synovitis. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11005148/
- People with CAI land more plantar-flexed and inverted (the classic "roll the ankle" position). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6922560/
- Rene-specific note: a **posteromedial talus avulsion** with severe swelling is more than a simple sprain. Persistent deep/medial ankle pain, catching, locking or swelling after 18+ months is a reason for a sports-med/foot-ankle review (possible osteochondral lesion, impingement, or ossicle) - exercise alone won't fix a mechanical block. The R-side calcification is typically benign (old ATFL/CFL injury) but confirms prior ligament damage.

### 1.2 What the 2021 JOSPT guideline says (evidence grades)
Grades: A strong, B moderate, C weak, D conflicting, E theoretical, F expert opinion.
- **Measure** (what to track): weight-bearing lunge test (dorsiflexion), single-limb balance eyes closed on firm surface, Star Excursion/Y-balance (anterior, anteromedial, posteromedial, posterolateral), timed single-limb hop tests. https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2021.0503
- **For CAI, strong-to-moderate evidence (A/B) supports: (1) proprioceptive and neuromuscular therapeutic exercise and (2) graded joint mobilizations.** Same source.
- A 4-week supervised program of balance + strengthening + functional tasks + ROM gave statistically and clinically significant benefit vs control (Tsikopoulos network MA, cited in CPG). https://www.jospt.org/doi/abs/10.2519/jospt.2021.0302
- External support (braces, insoles, tape) was **no better than control** for postural stability/balance in CAI (moderate evidence). Bracing still helps prevent re-sprain during sport (see below), but it doesn't retrain the ankle. Same source.

### 1.3 Each component and evidence
| Component | Evidence | Grade / strength | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Balance / proprioception training | Balance training alone reduced ankle sprain risk (RR 0.64); multi-intervention neuromuscular training halved ankle sprains (RR 0.50) | Strong (A) | Hübscher et al. 2010 systematic review https://europepmc.org/article/med/19952811 ; JOSPT CPG |
| Balance training in CAI | Improves CAIT self-reported function and dynamic balance (5 RCTs, I²=0% for CAIT) | Moderate-strong | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10860262 |
| Combined strength + balance | Beats either alone for self-reported function in CAI; balance adds more to dynamic balance than strength alone | Moderate | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11005148/ |
| Hop-stabilization training (6 wk, 18 sessions) | Lower ground reaction forces, more hip/knee flexion, less knee valgus on landing in CAI athletes; improves self-reported function | Moderate (RCTs) | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6922560/ ; https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/14/10/3502 |
| Joint mobilization / Mulligan mobilization with movement (MWM) | Moderately improves dorsiflexion ROM and dynamic postural control; MWM better than Maitland for dynamic balance; limited effect on patient-reported function alone | Moderate (B) | Vallandingham 2019 J Athl Train https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6522095/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9505831/ |
| Evertor (peroneal) + dorsiflexor strength | Evertor weakness strongly associated with CAI; bands/resistance recommended | Moderate | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11005148/ |
| Calf (plantarflexor) strength-endurance | Deficits common post-sprain; normal ~25 single-leg heel raises | Moderate (assessment); training supported by general rehab evidence | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11923623 |
| Tibialis anterior raises | Plausible (dorsiflexor strength, shin tolerance); **no direct CAI trial** found on "tib raises" as such | Weak / expert (E-F) | - |
| Brace/tape | Reduces re-sprain risk in sport; does not improve balance | Moderate for prevention; no rehab effect | JOSPT CPG |

### 1.4 Concrete 12-week ankle progression (fits into existing warm-ups)
Do 3x/week (e.g. before Legs A, Legs B, and on a golf/sport day). 10-15 min. Barefoot or flat shoes. Both sides, **worse side first and +1 set**. Progress when the current level is done with good control and pain ≤3/10 that settles by next morning.

**Phase 1 (weeks 1-3): open the joint + basic control**
1. Self-MWM dorsiflexion: band pulling the talus backward (band low on the front of the ankle, anchored behind) OR belt/strap; half-kneeling knee-over-toes lunge, 3 x 10 slow reps with 2-3 s hold at end range. Heel stays down, knee tracks over 2nd-3rd toe. (Already in plan - keep.)
2. Single-leg balance, firm floor: eyes open 3 x 30 s -> eyes closed 3 x 30 s. Goal: 30 s eyes closed with no touch-down.
3. Single-leg calf raise, straight knee, 3 x 12-15, slow (2 s up, 2 s down), full range off a step when tolerated. Then bent-knee (soleus) version 2 x 15.
4. Banded eversion (peroneals) 3 x 15-20 each side; banded dorsiflexion or tibialis raises (back against wall, heels ~30 cm out) 2 x 15-20.
5. Short foot / toe yoga 2 x 10 (weak evidence, low cost).

**Phase 2 (weeks 4-6): dynamic balance + load**
1. MWM continues, add load (dumbbell on knee) - "loaded deep split squats" in plan fit here.
2. Single-leg balance with perturbation: ball toss, head turns, reach with the free leg in the Y-balance directions (anterior, posteromedial, posterolateral) 3 x 5 each direction.
3. Single-leg calf raise weighted (hold DB, or Smith / seated calf machine) 3 x 8-12; aim for 25+ bodyweight single-leg reps on the endurance test.
4. Peroneal: heavy band eversion + single-leg balance on a slight lateral wedge; lateral band walks.
5. Double-leg pogo hops 3 x 20 s (stiff ankle, quiet landing), line hops forward/back and side to side 3 x 15 s.

**Phase 3 (weeks 7-9): hop-stabilization (the McKeon/Donovan style)**
1. Single-leg hop-and-stick: forward, lateral, medial, diagonal; hold landing 3 s. 3 x 5 each direction.
2. Hop to unstable-ish target (foam pad) then stick.
3. Lateral bounds (skater) with stick 3 x 5/side.
4. Single-leg pogo 3 x 10-15.
5. Calf: heavy (e.g. 1.5x BW seated / standing machine) 3 x 6-10 + tibialis raises weighted.

**Phase 4 (weeks 10-12+): sport-speed + return-to-play testing**
1. Continuous single-leg hops (side-to-side over a line, 30 s), figure-8 hops, cutting drills, deceleration.
2. Plyos: box jumps -> depth drops -> depth jumps (see section 4.5).
3. Re-test battery (section 3). Target: Y-balance anterior asymmetry <4 cm, composite ≥94% leg length, hop test limb symmetry ≥90%, knee-to-wall within ~1-2 cm side to side and ≥ ~9-10 cm, 25+ single-leg heel raises each side.
4. Maintenance: 2x/week 8-10 min balance + hops + calf forever (prevention effect disappears if you stop).

**Brace note:** during high-risk sports (basketball, soccer, tennis, pickup), a lace-up or semi-rigid brace is a reasonable, evidence-supported secondary prevention while rebuilding. Not needed for golf or lifting.

## 2. Golf-specific pain patterns + TPI-style self-screen

### 2.1 Where golfers hurt (epidemiology)
- Over half of golfers will have a musculoskeletal injury in their lifetime; **professionals are ~3x more likely than amateurs to have low back (RR 3.05) and hand/wrist (RR 3.33) injuries**. Most injuries are non-specific soft tissue. Robinson et al. 2024 SR/MA. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38508702/ (conference abstract: https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/58/Suppl_2/A66.2)
- Low back: most common site in both pros and amateurs; incidence 15-34% amateurs, 22-24% pros (McHardy 2007). https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S089934670700016X ; UK golfers: lower back 9.94 injuries/1000 h, most frequent site. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00913847.2024.2432859
- Risk factors for golf low back pain: previous back pain predicts future episodes (prospective); age and body mass (cross-sectional). Swing = repetitive, asymmetric, high angular velocity, high compressive/torsional/shear loads; big X-factor needs adequate spine/hip mobility. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6204638
- Lead hip: pros and tennis players with low back pain had **less lead-hip internal rotation** and a positive FABER test (Vad 2004, Am J Sports Med 32:494). Amateur golfers with LBP show the same lead-hip IR deficit (Murray 2009). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3772174 ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1466853X09000753
- Wrist: 13-20% of amateur and 20-27% of pro golf injuries; **lead wrist** more affected (ECU/FCU tendinopathy; hook-of-hamate fracture from grip/club butt, esp. fat shots). Elbow: medial ("golfer's elbow") and lateral epicondylalgia (lateral is actually more common in amateurs, lead arm). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1175860/ ; https://www.assh.org/handcare/condition/golf-injuries
- Shoulder: lead shoulder (posterior cuff, AC joint, labrum) in follow-through / cross-body adduction; trail shoulder in extreme ER/abduction at the top. https://openorthopaedicsjournal.com/VOLUME/17/ELOCATOR/e18743250262232/FULLTEXT/

**Mechanism summary for Rene (right-handed assumption; lead = left):** The swing needs rotation from the hips and thoracic spine. If lead-hip IR, trail-hip ER, or T-spine rotation is limited, the lumbar spine (only ~1-2 deg rotation per segment available) and the lead wrist/elbow absorb it. His **left ankle** is his lead ankle: poor dorsiflexion/stability on the lead side can limit posting/rotation into the lead hip in the downswing. Worth checking in video.

### 2.2 TPI Level 1 screen (self-run version) and scoring
TPI screen correlates with swing faults, not proven injury prediction. Research: toe touch fail linked to early extension (p=0.015); bridge fail linked to early extension and loss of posture; failed overhead deep squat or left single-leg balance = 2-3x more likely early extension / loss of posture / slide. Gulgin & Hoogenboom 2014. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24476744 . Several screens correlate with vertical jump / power. https://www.jptrs.org/journal/view.html?doi=10.14474%2Fptrs.2018.7.1.13 . Full list of tests: https://www.researchgate.net/figure/TPI-level-1-movement-screens_tbl1_259984831 and http://www.willbfitgolf.com/new-page-4

Film each from the side/front with a phone at hip height. Score **Pass / Limited / Fail** and note pain (P).

| Test | How | Pass | Fail / limited means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pelvic tilt | Golf posture, arms crossed on chest. Arch low back (anterior tilt) then flatten (posterior tilt) without moving chest/knees | Smooth both directions, no shaking | Can't do one direction or "shakes" = poor lumbopelvic control; linked to S-posture / C-posture, loss of posture |
| Pelvic rotation | Golf posture, arms crossed; rotate hips without turning shoulders | ~40-45 deg each way, smooth, shoulders still | Hips+shoulders move together = lower-body dissociation limit (hip IR / motor control) |
| Torso rotation | Golf posture, club across shoulders, hips kept still (or seated with club behind back); rotate shoulders | ≥45 deg each way (TPI target), club reaches past lead foot line | Limited T-spine rotation -> lumbar/shoulder overload |
| Overhead deep squat | Feet shoulder width, toes forward, dowel overhead arms locked, squat as deep as possible | Thighs below parallel, heels down, dowel over feet, torso parallel to shins | Heels up = ankle DF limit (expected for Rene); arms fall = lat/T-spine; retest with heels on 2" plate: if passes, ankle is the limiter |
| Toe touch | Feet together, straight knees, reach to toes | Touch toes, spine curves smoothly, hips shift back, weight neutral | Can't reach or flat spine = hamstring/neural or T-spine flexion limit -> early extension risk |
| 90/90 (shoulder ER) | Golf posture, arm at 90 deg abduction, elbow 90 deg, rotate back (top-of-backswing trail arm) | Forearm reaches ~parallel with spine angle (≈90 deg ER) without back arch | Limited trail-shoulder ER -> flying elbow, over-the-top |
| Single-leg balance | Arms at sides, lift one leg, eyes closed | ≥20-25 s eyes closed each leg | <10 s = balance/proprioception deficit (sway/slide); matches CAI deficits |
| Lat length | Back against wall, knees bent, low back flat, raise arms overhead | Thumbs touch wall with back flat | Lats short -> loss of posture, reverse spine |
| Lower quarter rotation | Stand, rotate trunk + hips fully, observe hip IR vs ER | ≥45-60 deg total each way, symmetric | Asymmetry left vs right = lead/trail hip limitation |
| Seated trunk rotation | Seated, club across chest, feet flat, rotate | ≥45 deg each way | Limited T-spine |
| Bridge with leg extension | Supine bridge, then straighten one knee keeping hips level, hold 10 s | Hips stay level 10 s, no cramping | Hip drops on the side of the extended leg = glute weakness on the standing side; hamstring/low-back cramp = compensatory recruitment. Source: https://www.mytpi.com/articles/screening/the-bridge-with-leg-extension-test |
| Reach-roll-lift | Kneel to child's pose, reach arm, roll palm up, lift | Arm lifts to horizontal | Scapular/T-spine control |
| Cervical rotation | Golf posture, rotate head, chin toward shoulder | Chin reaches ~70-80 deg (near shoulder) | Neck limit affects posture over ball |
| Wrist: hinge / flexion / extension / forearm rotation | Radial deviation, flexion, extension, supination/pronation | ~20 deg radial dev; ~60 deg flex/ext; 80-90 deg pronation/supination | Limit on lead wrist raises tendon load |

Useful TPI Level 2 power tests to self-track (same source): seated med-ball chest pass, sit-up throw, vertical jump, shot-put throw each side (rotational asymmetry).

## 3. Self-assessment battery (replaces the paid "movement and posture analysis")

Do at baseline, then every 4-6 weeks. Same shoes (barefoot), same time of day, warmed-up light. Record numbers + photos in one sheet. Asymmetry matters more than any one number.

### 3.1 Photos / video (smartphone on tripod at hip height, 3 m away)
| Capture | How | What to look at (and what NOT to overinterpret) |
|---|---|---|
| Standing posture front/side/back | Relaxed stance, shorts, no shirt, feet hip width | Obvious side-to-side shift, shoulder/hip height difference, foot turn-out asymmetry (R vs L), knee alignment. Evidence: posture/pelvic asymmetry is weakly or not associated with pain; 85% of asymptomatic men have anterior pelvic tilt (mean ~6-7 deg). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21658988 ; pelvic asymmetry not meaningfully associated with LBP; posture-LBP link heterogeneous https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39166267 . Use photos to track change, not to diagnose. |
| Gait | Walk 10 m barefoot, film from side + behind at 60/120 fps slow-mo | Symmetric step length, heel-strike to toe-off roll, one side "vaulting" or out-toeing more, limp. Early heel lift on one side = DF limit on that side. |
| Single-leg squat (to ~60 deg knee bend) x5 each | Arms crossed, film from front | Knee drifting inward past big toe, pelvis drop, trunk lean, foot arch collapse, wobble. Dynamic valgus in landing is a real ACL risk marker (Hewett 2005 in female athletes) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15722287 . Score: good/fair/poor per side. |
| Overhead squat | As in TPI above, film front + side | Heel rise, knee cave, trunk lean, arm drop, shift to one side (common with R/L asymmetry) |
| Drop-landing / hop-and-stick | Step off 30 cm box to two feet, then single-leg | Quiet landing, knees over toes, no inward collapse, stick within 1-2 s |

### 3.2 Measured tests with targets
| Test | Protocol | Normal / target | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Knee-to-wall (weight-bearing lunge)** | Barefoot, big toe behind a line, heel down, knee touches wall; slide foot back until max distance where heel stays down + knee touches. Clinical rule of thumb ≥10 cm. **Side-to-side difference >1.5-2 cm** is meaningful (Hoch 2011 asymmetry norms). Target for Rene: ≥10 cm and <2 cm asymmetry before barbell back squat. | https://www.mskscienceandpractice.com/article/S2468-7812(26)00040-8/fulltext ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21429784 |
| **Single-leg heel raise to fatigue** | Barefoot, 10 deg incline (or flat), straight knee, fingertip support, 1 rep per 2 s (metronome), max height each rep; stop when height drops / tempo lost | Healthy men median ~26 reps (95% range 14-53); classic "normal" = 25. Target: ≥25 each side and <10% side difference. | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11923623 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28886865 |
| **Single-leg balance eyes closed** | Firm floor, hands on hips, 3 trials | ≥20-30 s each leg; JOSPT CPG recommends as CAI measure | https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2021.0503 |
| **Y-balance / Star Excursion** | Tape 3 lines (anterior, posteromedial, posterolateral). Stand on one leg, reach max with other foot, touch lightly, return. | **Hip IR / ER** | Seated at edge of table, knees 90 deg, rotate shin out (IR) and in (ER); phone inclinometer app on shin. Thigh up = iliopsoas short; knee straightens = rectus femoris short; thigh abducts = TFL/ITB | https://www.physio-pedia.com/Thomas_Test ; https://www.physio-pedia.com/Range_of_Motion_Normative_Values |

| **Side plank endurance** (McGill) | Time to failure each side | Healthy young men ~95-100 s each side; right:left ratio 0.95-1.05 (>0.05 asymmetry associated with back issues in McGill's normative work) | McGill core norms (Strength Cond J 2010; ratio guidance widely cited) |
| **Pain log** | Rate worst pain 0-10 during & next morning for each sport/lift | See section 5 | - |

**Suggested re-test cadence:** baseline week 0, week 6, week 12. Priorities for Rene: knee-to-wall (both), heel raise count, eyes-closed balance, Y-balance, hop LSI, lead hip IR, torso rotation.

## 4. Athletic lifting for a tall lifter: add resilience + power to the hypertrophy split

### 4.1 Why keep lifting (counter to "GOATA doesn't favor lifting")
- **Strength training reduced sports injuries to <1/3 (RR ~0.32) and nearly halved overuse injuries; stretching had no effect (RR 0.96).** Lauersen 2014 BJSM MA, 25 RCTs, 26,610 people. https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/48/11/871 ; 2018 follow-up RR 0.338, dose-dependent (10% more strength volume -> >4 percentage-point lower risk), high strength of evidence. https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/52/24/1557
- Golf: strength + golf-specific training raises clubhead speed ~4.1% and distance ~5.2% (vs 1.6%/4.8% for general strength alone). Ehlert/Uthoff SR, JSCR 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34224506 . Jumps, throws, squat/bench 1RM correlate with clubhead speed; +17 m drive ≈ 0.75-2.2 strokes/round. https://eleiko.com/sv/stories/strength-and-power-are-a-driving-force-in-golf
- Bottom line: the hypertrophy split is an asset. The gap is **single-leg control, elastic/reactive ability, rotation power, and ankle/foot capacity**, not "too much lifting".

### 4.2 How to add athletic work without adding days (fits the existing 5-day split)
Principle: 15-20 min "athletic block" at the **start** of 3 sessions (after warm-up, before hypertrophy work) when fresh. Trim 1-2 isolation sets per session to keep 60-75 min.

| Day (current) | Add at start (athletic block) | Swap / add in main work |
|---|---|---|
| Mon Push | Med-ball rotational scoop toss 3x5/side + med-ball chest pass 3x5 (TPI power test mirror) | Add half-kneeling landmine press (single-arm, anti-rotation) as a press option |
| Tue Legs A (quad) | Ankle block (section 1.4) + pogo/line hops + hop-and-stick (phase-appropriate) | Keep hack/pendulum + Bulgarian split squat. Add **slant-board / heel-elevated split squat** (knee travels over toe) and **tibialis raises** 2x15-20; **reverse Nordic** 2x6-8 (partial range first) |
| Wed Pull | Med-ball shot-put throw / rotational slam 3x5/side | Add **dead hang** 2-3 x 30-60 s (shoulder/spine decompression, grip); single-arm cable row with rotation |
| Thu Legs B (ham/glute) | Ankle block + lateral bounds with stick, box jumps (step down) | **Nordic hamstring** 2-3 x 3-6 (eccentric focus) or Nordic-regression machine/band-assisted; **Copenhagen side plank** 2-3 x 10-20 s/side (short lever first); single-leg RDL; hip thrust; **sled push/pull** 4-6 x 20 m (knee-friendly, ankle DF under load) |
| Fri Upper + cardio | Med-ball throws (light) | Farmer carry / suitcase carry 3 x 30-40 m (anti-lateral flexion, grip, ribs-to-pelvis bracing); Pallof press |
| Sat Pilates | Keep - good for trunk control and hip dissociation | - |

Evidence notes:
- **Nordic hamstring exercise**: programs including it **halve hamstring injuries (RR 0.49)**, 15 studies, 8,459 athletes. van Dyk 2019 BJSM. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30808663/ (Caveat: independent effect debated in later reappraisal; still a low-cost, strong-signal addition.) Volume can be low: 2 sets x 4-6, 1-2x/week, maintains fascicle gains (which reverse within 2 wks of stopping).
- **Copenhagen adduction**: reliably increases eccentric adductor strength (~+8% in 8 wk); a 2025 SR/MA found the isolated injury-reduction effect nonsignificant (RR 0.83, very low certainty), while earlier RCTs (Harøy 2019) showed ~41% fewer groin problems in soccer. Grade: good for adductor strength; groin-injury prevention modest/uncertain. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363431 ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666506926000027
- **Knees over toes**: Restricting knee travel in squats shifts torque to hips/low back (Fry 2003). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14636100 . Knees-over-toes training is a valid tool (deep knee flexion is trainable), but "just another tool" - not a magic system. https://www.sportsmith.co/articles/knees-over-toes/ . Tibialis raises / reverse Nordics / ATG split squats: good low-risk ideas, but **no RCT evidence** that the specific KOT program prevents injury (Grade E-F). Use them as loading tools for the knee/shin/ankle.
- **Plyometrics**: beginner 50-80 foot contacts/session, 2x/week, 48 h apart; intermediate 80-120; advanced up to 140 high-intensity. Mostly Level 5 evidence for dosing, but good evidence for performance benefit; >80 contacts/session best for sprint gains. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4637913
- **Hop-stabilization** (section 1) doubles as the plyo on-ramp for his ankles.

### 4.3 Plyo / jump progression (tie to ankle phases)
1. Weeks 1-3: snap-downs, squat jump with stick, box jump (step down), low pogo 2-leg. ~40-60 contacts.
2. Weeks 4-6: continuous pogos, broad jump + stick, lateral line hops, skater bound with stick. ~60-80.
3. Weeks 7-9: single-leg hop-and-stick multi-direction, low drop landing (20-30 cm) -> drop jump, hurdle hops. ~80-100.
4. Weeks 10+: depth jumps (30-45 cm), single-leg bounds, reactive lateral bounds, sprint accelerations. ~100-120 with 2x/week cap.
Gate to progress: pain ≤3/10 during and settled next morning; landings quiet with no knee cave on video; passing previous-phase hop tests.

### 4.4 Rotational power for golf
- Med-ball scoop toss (hip-to-wall), shot-put throw, overhead slam, rotational slam: 3-4 sets x 3-5 reps/side, max intent, full rest (30-60 s), 2-3x/week. 2-4 kg ball (heavier = slower; speed matters).
- Always train **both sides** (the swing is one-sided; counter-rotation helps symmetry), weighted toward the swing side for performance.
- Cable/landmine lift and chop, Pallof press = anti-rotation / trunk stiffness (McGill-style bracing).
- Hip IR/ER mobility and strength (90/90 lift-offs, hip CARs, seated IR with band) especially **lead hip IR**, which is directly linked to golfer LBP.
- Thoracic rotation drills (open books, quadruped T-spine rotation).

### 4.5 Technique principles for pain-free lifting (tall lifter)
- **Tall-lifter reality:** long femurs/tibias = more forward trunk lean in squats, longer ROM in deadlifts/pressing. That's anatomy, not a fault. Pick variations that fit: hack/pendulum/belt squat, safety-bar squat, front-foot-elevated split squats, trap-bar deadlift, RDL, block pulls if hips round early. Wider stance + slight toe-out is normal for long femurs.
- **Barbell back squat ban:** reasonable as a temporary gate while ankle DF is limited, but not required forever. Heel-elevated squats (plates/wedge) are fine and do not "hurt" the ankle; they let him squat while mobility work continues. Re-test: knee-to-wall ≥10 cm and <2 cm asymmetry + pain-free overhead squat with heels down -> reintroduce back squat with light loads.
- **Foot tripod:** weight spread across big toe ball, little toe ball, heel; arch "active" but not rigid. Knees track roughly over 2nd-3rd toes; some knee in-out movement under max effort is normal. (Consistent with GOATA's "don't collapse" idea only in the moderate form.)
- **Bracing:** breathe into the belly/360 deg, then brace ~ "cough" stiffness before each rep for heavy lifts (McGill/Starrett approach). Ribs stacked over pelvis. Belt optional for top sets.
- **Tempo:** 2-3 s eccentric, brief pause in the bottom for joint-irritated lifts (increases control, lowers load needed). Isometric holds (30-45 s at 70% effort) as short-term pain relief for irritated tendons; heavy slow resistance (3-4 s up/down) for long-term tendon capacity (patellar tendinopathy SR). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29972281/
- **Range:** use the biggest range you can control pain-free; shorten range temporarily when a joint is irritated, then extend over weeks. Don't chase "perfect" depth if heels lift.
- **Unilateral first when asymmetric:** start sets with the weaker side, match reps on the strong side. Use split squats, single-leg RDL, single-arm rows/presses. Track side differences.
- **Load management:** keep 1-3 reps in reserve most sets; avoid spikes (>10-20% week-to-week jumps in total lower-body + sport load), especially when adding plyos and sport at the same time (IOC load consensus). https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/50/17/1030
- **Core:** McGill Big 3 (modified curl-up, side bridge, bird dog) = low-spine-load stiffness drills; good warm-up for golfers with LBP history. https://squatuniversity.com/2018/06/21/the-mcgill-big-3-for-core-stability

## 5. Pain science: training through pain, load management, red flags

### 5.1 Is it OK to train with pain?
- **Exercising into some pain is not harmful and may help a bit.** Smith 2017 BJSM SR/MA: painful exercise protocols gave a small short-term benefit (SMD -0.27) over pain-free exercise for chronic MSK pain; no difference medium/long term. "Pain during therapeutic exercise need not be a barrier." https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28596288/ ; https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/51/23/1679
- **Pain-monitoring model (Silbernagel 2007 RCT, Achilles):** continuing running/jumping with pain monitoring gave the same good outcomes as rest with no negative effects. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17307888 

### 5.2 The rules (use for any sport, lift, or rehab exercise)
Scale 0 = no pain, 10 = worst imaginable.
| Zone | Pain | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Green (safe) | 0-2/10 | Keep going, progress |
| Yellow (acceptable) | 3-5/10 during or right after | OK **if**: (1) it settles back to baseline by next morning (within 24 h), (2) morning pain/stiffness isn't worse, (3) pain isn't trending up week to week |
| Red (high risk) | >5/10, or next-morning pain higher than usual, or rising week to week | Reduce that activity's load 20-30% (weight, reps, range, speed, jumps, holes played) or swap the exercise; re-test in 2-3 days |
Sources: https://physicaltherapyfirst.com/blog/continued-sports-activity-using-a-pain-monitoring-model-during-rehabilitation-in-patients-with-achilles-tendinopathy/ ; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/6497499 ; next-day check emphasized in 2025 BJSM Delphi https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/59/19/1337
- Conservative rule for Rene's **joints** (ankle, knee, back): aim to stay ≤3/10 (Green-Yellow), since 5/10 comes from tendon rehab. Sharp/pinching joint pain is different from dull achy tendon/muscle discomfort - modify on sharp pain.
- Track in a simple log: activity, worst pain, next-morning pain. Pattern > single day.

### 5.3 Load management
- IOC consensus: injury risk rises with rapid spikes in load and poor recovery; sufficient, gradually built chronic load is protective. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27535989
- Practical: change only one big variable at a time (e.g. don't start plyos, a new sport, and heavier legs the same week). Increase total weekly jumps/sport volume ~10%/week. Deload every 4-6 weeks (cut volume 30-50%). Sleep + protein already covered in his plan.

### 5.4 When to see a doctor / PT (red flags)
**Go to ER / same day:**
- New bladder/bowel problems, numbness in the saddle/groin area, progressive leg weakness, both-leg sciatica (cauda equina). https://www.aafp.org/afp/2023/0400/practice-guidelines-low-back-pain ; https://apps.nhslothian.scot/refhelp/guidelines/musculoskeletalphysiotherapy/lumbarspine/serious-lumbar-spine-conditions-red-flags/
- Fever with back/joint pain, a hot swollen joint, unexplained weight loss, pain after significant trauma, can't bear weight (Ottawa rules for ankle: can't take 4 steps, bone tenderness at the back edge/tip of either malleolus or base of 5th metatarsal/navicular). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27376890

**Book sports-med / PT soon (within 1-2 weeks):**
- Ankle "giving way", catching, locking, or recurrent swelling (possible osteochondral lesion of the talus or loose body - relevant given his talus avulsion history).
- Deep ankle pain that doesn't improve after 6-8 weeks of the program.
- Low back pain that radiates below the knee, pain on one-leg standing back extension (stork test - spondylolysis in rotational athletes), night pain, or no improvement after 4-6 weeks.
- Lead-wrist pain on the pinky side / heel of the hand that is point-tender (hook of hamate, ECU tendon) - these can need imaging.
- Hip/groin pain with clicking, pinching in deep flexion + rotation (possible FAI/labrum; common in golf/rotational sports).
- Pain that consistently exceeds 5/10 or keeps rising despite load reduction.

**Recommended one-time spend (for Rene to decide, not purchased):** one evaluation with a sports-physio or TPI-certified PT (often $150-250) likely gives more than a $2,500 10-week "movement" package, because it includes a hands-on joint exam of the ankles.

## 6. GOATA claims vs evidence

GOATA key points (from their own material, see 21_goata_raw_sources.md): keep inner ankle bones high / don't let feet collapse / wean off orthotics; "set the bows" (legs as springs, kneecaps slightly out, second toe forward); "corner the hips"; heel/posterior-chain emphasis in gait; "the squat is the landing"; skepticism of traditional lifting ("we don't train the lifting engine"); indigenous-movement ideal; anterior pelvic tilt treated as natural.

| GOATA claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Foot/ankle is foundational; train it; wean off unneeded support | **Mostly consistent** | Ankle/foot neuromuscular training is Grade A-B for CAI and halves ankle sprains. External supports (orthotics, tape, brace) don't improve balance in CAI. https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2021.0503 . But: braces DO reduce re-sprains in high-risk sport - don't throw them away mid-rehab. |
| Posterior chain emphasis (glutes, hamstrings, calves) | **Consistent** | Nordic hamstring halves hamstring injuries; calf capacity central to ankle rehab; glute/hip strength linked to lower-limb control. |
| Gait / walking focus, athletic movement over isolated machines | **Partly consistent** | Movement-specific and multi-component training beats single-mode; but there's no evidence for one "correct" gait pattern for injury prevention, and gait retraining is only evidence-based for specific conditions (e.g., running-related knee pain). |
| "No inside ankle collapse" / arches should never collapse / pronation is bad | **Not supported as stated** | Pronation is a normal shock-absorbing motion. In 927 novice runners, pronated feet had **no higher injury risk** (even slightly fewer injuries/1000 km) than neutral. Nielsen 2014 BJSM. https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/48/6/440 . What IS supported: uncontrolled **dynamic knee valgus** in landing raises ACL risk (Hewett 2005 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15722287) -> train landing control, don't fear normal pronation. |
| Kneecaps slightly out, "second toe forward", one ideal squat shape | **Not supported as universal** | Hip/femur anatomy varies (femoral torsion, hip socket depth); stance and toe angle should be individualized. Knee tracking over the foot is reasonable; a single mandated shape is not evidence-based. |
| Anterior pelvic tilt is normal | **Consistent** | 85% of asymptomatic men have anterior tilt (~6-7 deg). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21658988 . Posture/pelvic asymmetry weakly or not linked to LBP. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39166267 |
| Heavy lifting isn't needed / "don't train the lifting engine" | **Contradicted** | Strength training cuts sports injuries to <1/3, dose-dependent; improves golf clubhead speed. https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/48/11/871 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34224506 |
| Posture "decompressing"/"unlocking" over 10 weeks via posture correction | **Not supported** | Posture correction alone has weak links to pain outcomes; improvements more plausibly from graded exercise and reassurance. |
| Indigenous-movement ideal | **Not evidence** | Anecdotal/appeal-to-nature; no outcome data. |
| Independent evaluation of GOATA as a system | **None exists** | No peer-reviewed trials of GOATA. Critiques by physios in 21_goata_raw_sources.md (YouTube transcripts). Treat GOATA cues as optional coaching cues, not as treatment. |

**Net recommendation for the final plan:** keep the bodybuilding split (it's protective); add the ankle CAI progression + hop-stabilization, single-leg + posterior-chain + rotational power work above; use GOATA-style foot awareness cues (tripod foot, active arch, knee over toes in landing) only as cues; use the section 3 battery every 6 weeks to steer; get one hands-on sports-PT/foot-ankle check for the left talus history if pain/catching persists.
