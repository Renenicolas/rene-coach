## Lesson 2: The movement idea (GOATA in plain words)

**The big idea:** stand and move with your **hips back and head forward**, keep your **arch up with the big toe down**, and let your **knee point out over your little toes** when you land. Train those shapes slowly first, then add load and speed.

**What they teach:**
- **GOATA's 7 "laws":**
  - Back chain first: hips behind the ribs.
  - Feet about a fist apart.
  - "Inside ankle bone high": weight on the ball and outer edge of the foot, light heel.
  - The "bow": the knee opens outward when you land.
  - The "corner": the knee closes back in when you push off.
  - The recoil: the foot releases and resets.
  - Head over the working foot.
- **Tripp's version:** "Load down, back and out; release up and in." "Pain is a loading problem, not necessarily a weakness problem." He blames stiff ankles for feet turning out, which overloads the knees, hips and back.
- **Mucci and Mitchell:** "stop duck walking." Feet straight or slightly in, hips stacked over ankles, head back, shoulders back and down, walk "in a spiral."

**What holds up:**
- **Solid:** keeping the knee from caving inward on landings and cuts. That's the core of every ACL-prevention program. Strong feet and big-toe control also help balance.
- **Solid:** a slightly forward pelvis (anterior tilt) is normal. About 85% of healthy men have it, so there's nothing to "fix."
- **Not supported:** "bad posture causes pain." Long-term studies don't show it. Forcing ruler-straight or toes-in feet isn't supported either, because some toe-out comes from your bone shape.
- **Not supported:** walking on light heels all day. Heel-first walking is actually more efficient.
- **Your risk:** "inside ankle bone high" puts weight on the outside edge of the foot. With your two old ankle injuries, that's close to the rolled-ankle position. **Use it in slow drills only, never for landing or cutting in sport.**

**What you'll do:** one cue, everywhere: **"knee over little toes, big toe down, arch up."** Check it on film, from the front, on your split squats and landings.

**Watch (1 min):** Tripp's split-stance "absorb it" superset, instagram.com/p/DcBaUtUhI7n. Listen for "keep all the toes down... keep the ankle in the right spot."

**Decide:** will you try GOATA's golf setup (feet a bit closer, less toe flare) on the range, filmed, and judge it by ball flight and pain?

## Lesson 3: Your ankles (the most important lesson)

**The big idea:** your old left talus fracture (2024) and right ligament injury (2025) are probably behind a lot of your pain up the chain. About 40% of people still have wobbly ankles a year after a bad sprain. What fixes it best is **balance training plus joint mobility plus calf and outer-ankle strength**, not GOATA positions.

**What they teach:**
- **Tripp:** a stiff ankle makes you compensate everywhere else. Toe-tuck rockers and slant boards "open" it.
- **Mucci's "Fix the Fascia" (in Kaya):** seiza rockers with toes in and heels out, flat-to-toes transitions, deep-squat calf raises, the "bow" wall hold.
- **GOATA:** slant boards, "Chucks" heel blocks, holds on the ball of the foot.

**What holds up:**
- **Solid:** balance training cut ankle sprains by about 46% in a big review of 3,577 athletes. Exercise rehab cut repeat sprains by about 40%. Ankle mobilization while moving improves ankle bend.
- **Risky for you:** Mucci moves 1-3 (toes in, heels out, outer ankle bone toward the floor) put the ankle in **pointed plus rolled-in**, the exact position that sprains the outer ligaments. Moves 4, 5, 7 and 8 need deep ankle bend you don't have yet.

**What you'll do (12 weeks, before leg days, 10-15 min, worse ankle first):**
| Weeks | Do |
|---|---|
| 1-3 | Band ankle mobilization 3x10; single-leg balance, eyes open then closed, 3x30 sec; slow single-leg calf raises 3x12-15; band eversion (foot pushes outward against a band) 3x15-20; tibialis raises 2x15-20 |
| 4-6 | Balance with ball tosses and head turns; weighted calf raises; double-leg pogo hops; line hops |
| 7-9 | Single-leg hop and stick in 4 directions; skater bounds with a stick landing; heavier calf raises |
| 10-12 | Side-to-side hops, figure-8 hops, cutting drills; retest everything |
| After | 2x a week, 8-10 min, forever. The protection fades if you stop. |

- **Mucci's moves, done safely:** only after week 4. Put a towel under your ankles, keep weight on your hands, do 20-30 sec instead of 2 min, and keep your feet straight rather than turned in. Stop at any outer-ankle pain.
- **Brace:** wear a lace-up brace for pickup basketball, soccer or tennis while you rebuild.
- **New from Mitchell's 2nd account:**
  - **Backwards treadmill walking, 5-10 min before leg days.** Start flat, then add incline. He calls it the next best thing to a sled for knees, shins and ankles. Low impact, so it's a good fit for you.
  - **Single-leg calf raises on a slant board, slow.** Start on flat ground; add the slant once your knee-to-wall number improves.

**Watch:** none needed; these are standard moves. I'll show you a clip only if one is unclear.

**Decide:**
- Will you do the Week 0 test (knee-to-wall in cm, calf-raise count, eyes-closed balance)?
- Do you want the one-time sports PT visit (~$150-250) to check what's blocking the left ankle? A catching or locking ankle after a talus avulsion can be a cartilage problem that exercise won't fix.

## Lesson 4: The daily floor routine (the GOATA "Recode," free)

**The big idea:** 10 minutes on the floor every morning is the heart of what Tripp sells for $2,500 and GOATA sells for $599. It's low-risk and it helps range.

**What they teach:**
- **Tripp's 30-day morning routine** (his post DTyX8FYkdMI):
  - Back breathing on your back, legs up, 2 min.
  - Table-top rock-back, 30.
  - Toes-tucked rock-back, 30.
  - Table-top leg lift with inward rotation, 20/side.
  - Back leg lengthened behind you, 45 sec/side.
- **More Tripp:** crawl 3-5 min daily; crawl 10-20 min on recovery days. "Tummy time"/supermans instead of "spamming planks." Floor sitting as your "default settings."
- **GOATA Phase 1:** child rocker, toe-tuck rocker, bow and corner hip stretches, baby crawl.
- **Mitchell:** "I always hated stretching, but I made sure every morning or evening I stuck with my mobility routine."

**Real example:** Mitchell did 35 days of two sessions a day, one of them mobility. His result: "I was always very one-sided, like I could do a split, but I couldn't touch my toes until now."

**What holds up:** plausible and low-risk. Rocking, crawling and floor sitting build hip, knee and ankle range. The "decompression" and "fascia" explanations are marketing.

**What you'll do (every morning after your walk, ~10 min):**
1. Back breathing, 1-2 min.
2. Rock-backs, 30.
3. Toes-tucked rock-backs, 20-30, with a pillow under your knees if your ankles complain.
4. Inward-rotation leg lifts, 10-20/side. This trains lead-hip rotation, which golfers need.
5. Hip-flexor stretch with the back leg long, 45 sec/side.
6. Crawl, 1-2 min.
7. Supermans, 15.

**Evening option (Mitchell's "if you're sitting all day" routine):**
- Couch stretch, 1 min/side.
- Elevated pigeon, 1 min/side.
- Deep squat, 2 min, holding a pole with a heel lift.
- Elephant walks, 20/side.
- Butterfly against a wall, 2 min.

**Watch (1 min):** Tripp's morning routine, instagram.com/p/DTyX8FYkdMI. It's music only; the moves are on screen.

**Decide:** morning or night? Tripp says morning. Mitchell's Kaya routine is either.

## Lesson 5: Lifting: keep it, upgrade it

**The big idea:** **don't quit lifting.** Strength training cuts sports injuries to under a third in large studies. Borrow their athletic swaps as extras.

**What they teach:**
- **Tripp and GOATA:** no barbell squat, deadlift, bench, hip thrusts or Olympic lifts. Use instead:
  - Slant-board isometric holds "until you shake."
  - Rear-foot-elevated split-squat holds.
  - Landmine presses from a split stance ("get off your back and onto your feet").
  - Sled pushes and walking lunges instead of deadlifts.
  - A kneeling band circuit for the shoulders.
- **Mitchell:** lift hard.
  - "Two leg exercises, both until failure, one or two sets each," then 2 chest and 2 back movements.
  - Dips "lean forward... work the lower chest."
  - 6-10 reps, 1-2 reps short of failure, 3-6 days a week.
  - Core trained with weight: side bends, cable crunches, hanging leg raises.
  - Sprints 1-2x a week: 6-10 x 15-30 sec.
  - Back extensions for hunched shoulders.

**What holds up:**
- **Solid:** Mitchell's approach, and Tripp's split-squat holds, sled and landmine work.
- **Not supported:** GOATA's "heavy lifting ruins athletes."
- **Solid (your own plan already agrees):** at 6'4", hack, pendulum, heel-elevated and split squats suit your build.

**What you'll do:** keep your Superpower 5-day split, and add a 10-15 min athletic block at the start of lift days:
- **Mon:** med-ball rotational throws.
- **Tue:** ankle work + hops. Add tibialis raises and reverse Nordics.
- **Wed:** med-ball shot-put throws; longer dead hangs.
- **Thu:** ankle work + hop and stick. Add Nordic curls, Copenhagen side planks, and a sled push finisher.
- **Fri:** suitcase carries, Pallof press.

**Mitchell's joint-friendly athletic moves (from his 2nd account), pick 2-3:**
- ATG split squat at 4-2-1 tempo (4 sec down, 2 sec pause, 1 sec up).
- Weighted Poliquin step-downs (knees).
- Copenhagen plank into a rear-delt raise.
- Back extensions.
- Suitcase-carry incline walk. Switch hands every 2-5 min; great trunk work for golf.
- He also lists what he calls the 4-5 things everyone should do: lift, sprint, walk, mobility, easy zone 2.

**Mitchell's on-screen workouts (from his music-only reels):**
- **Knee-friendly leg day:**
  - Box jumps.
  - Front squat.
  - Goblet squat.
  - Peterson step-down: stand on a step, heel of the working leg stays down, knee travels forward over the toes.
  - Dumbbell single-leg RDL.
- **Core, not "abs":**
  - L-sit hold.
  - Hanging leg raises: no swinging, straight legs.
  - Banded front folds: lying down, pull a band into a V-up, 20 reps.
  - Great for golf.
- **Pull day:**
  - Pull-ups 4x10-12.
  - Single-arm dumbbell row 4x10-15.
  - Half-kneeling single-arm high cable row 4x10-15.
  - Seated cable row 3x8-12.
  - Face pulls 3x12-15.
  - Hammer curls 3x10-15.
  - Barbell 21s x2.

**Optional Tripp swaps:**
- Split-stance landmine press for 1-2 fly/pec-deck sets.
- A 20-45 sec rear-foot-elevated split-squat hold at the end of leg days.

**Watch (1 min):** Tripp's standing press, instagram.com/p/DcEHemjs9PS ("hips are back, the foot is completely active").

**Decide:** which optional swaps do you want in, if any?

## Lesson 6: Walking, cardio and golf

**The big idea:** walking is the most underrated tool in all of this. Everyone agrees on it.

**What they teach:**
- **Mitchell:** "walking is magical... 10k steps a day, you burn 400 to 500 calories."
  - He paces around the kitchen island on calls and always takes the stairs.
  - Walks 10-15 min after meals.
  - Keeps runs to zone 2, "10-minute miles," no more than 4-5 miles, because long running costs muscle and makes you hungry.
- **Tripp:** walking is where your pattern lives (150 million steps in a lifetime); walk barefoot on grass when you can.
- **GOATA golf:** Ricky Stanzi's golf podcast (Golf 360 #097) and the "Floor Level Swing" video.

**What holds up:**
- **Solid:** steps, walking after meals, zone 2 cardio.
- **Solid:** golf research says strength plus rotation power adds about 4% clubhead speed.
- **Solid:** limited inward rotation in the lead hip is tied to golfers' low-back pain.

**What you'll do:**
- 10-12k steps a day, and walk 10 min after dinner.
- Walk the course when you play.
- Med-ball rotational throws 2-3x a week (in Lesson 5).
- Hip-rotation drills daily (in Lesson 4).

**Mental rehearsal (from a magazine profile of Mitchell):**
- "By the time he steps onto the strip, the match has already played out, dozens of times... sketched in the margins of his notebook."
- Do the same the night before a round: write out and picture your key holes and shots. Visualization is well supported in sport psychology.

**Tournament travel (his "16 travel rules"):**
- Don't eat on the plane.
- Get sunlight and a walk right after landing.
- 10-20k steps a day.
- A quick lift to "use the fuel."
- When eating out, ask: "no oil? no sugar? sauce on the side?"
- Green tea before deciding if you're hungry.
- Have the treat first, then stop.
- Rest.

**Decide:** are you right-handed? Then your left is your lead side, and your left ankle and hip matter most for the swing.

## Lesson 7: Food and metabolism

**The big idea:** your metabolism is fine (TSH (normal), glucose (normal), triglycerides (normal)). "Boosting metabolism" really means: build muscle, walk more, eat enough protein and real food, sleep, and drink less.

**What they teach:**
- **Tripp:** "sugar by day, protein at night."
  - Fruit, juice, honey, Coke, ice cream, gelatin and milk all day; one 100 g protein meal at dinner.
  - 400-500 g sugar a day; no fasting; no fish oil.
  - Taurine 8-10 g before going out.
- **Mitchell (now):** single-ingredient whole foods, protein first at every meal (35-50 g at the first meal, ~1 g per lb a day).
  - High-protein, higher-carb, low-fat meal before training.
  - Fruit, honey and white rice are fine. Bone-in sardines are a staple.
  - "Fat loss is about a 500 kcal deficit"; don't count calories at first, but "it's thermodynamics."
- **Mitchell (2024, old):** strict animal-based with raw dairy. He has since moved away from it.

**What holds up:**
- **Mitchell's current approach is solid** and matches your plan almost exactly.
- **Tripp's is the weakest part of everything we reviewed:** it would cut your protein from 200 g to about 120 g and leave you 8 hours after lifting with no protein. Fish-oil and seed-oil fears aren't supported. Body temperature as a thyroid test is rejected by the American Thyroid Association.

**What you'll do:**
- **No change** to your 200 g protein / ~2,750 kcal plan.
- **Borrow from both:**
  - Fruit, OJ or honey around training (same total carbs).
  - A Greek yogurt or cottage cheese bowl with fruit and honey at night (Kaya's "bowl season"), taken out of meal 3.
  - Sardines or salmon several times a week.
  - Oysters weekly for copper.
  - Collagen 10-15 g with vitamin C 30-60 min before ankle work. You already take collagen, so just time it.

**New from Mitchell's 2nd account:**
- **Don't "mini cut" after a trip.** "The scale and mirror lie that first week after travel."
- **Cuts take 3-6 months, then take a break.**
- **Track calories for one week to calibrate, then eyeball.** Most people undereat by day and overeat at night.
- **After a trip, wait a week before judging.** "100 grams of carbs... can literally hold 400 grams of water." Go back to maintenance and your normal lifting. No fasting.
- **Tournament days:** keep fat low and eat easy carbs (fruit, honey, rice) between rounds, like he does on competition days.
- **The binge-day math (his slides):** a 10,000-calorie day added only about **1-1.3 lb of actual fat**, even though the scale jumped 5-12 lb. The rest was water, stored carbs, sodium and food in the gut.
  - To limit it: lift and walk that day, keep the excess mostly carbs and protein (not fat), then go straight back to normal.
  - This is consistent with overfeeding research.
- **Eat your plate in order: veggies/fiber, then protein, then carbs** (his "natural GLP-1" slide). Small trials show this lowers blood sugar spikes and keeps you fuller. Easy win: eat the veg and meat before the rice.
- **Eat until about 80% full** (the Japanese "hara hachi bu" rule; also his 2026 rule, "always stop before full").
- **Stay lean year-round instead of bulk/cut.** That matches your recomp plan.
- **Eating-out check:** where's the protein, will it keep me full, how will I feel in 2 hours?

**Decide:** do you want to cut or keep your weight where it is? That sets your calories.

## Lesson 8: Sun, sleep and stress (Mitchell's real core)

**The big idea:** this is the part Mitchell is genuinely best at, and it's all free.

**What they teach:**
- **Mitchell:**
  - Outside within 10-30 min of waking, 5-20 min, "even on a cloudy day." Indoor light is only 50-1,000 lux.
  - After sunset, dim warm lights, phone on red/night mode, phone out of the bedroom.
  - Room at 67-69°F and pitch black; same bedtime every night.
  - Stop eating 1.5-3 h before bed; hot shower 1-2 h before.
  - 8-9 h of sleep, "treat it as training."
  - Magnesium glycinate ~300 mg plus glycine 3 g at night.
  - Caffeine cutoff early afternoon.
  - Salt your morning water.
  - Airplane-mode mornings, meals without screens.
  - His acne and injuries cleared when stress dropped.
- **Tripp:** morning sun, warm light at night, walks after meals.

**What holds up:** solid, except the extras (grounding "electrons," structured water, EMF blockers), which are unproven. Mitchell admits much of that is satire.

**Breathwork (the 6 Kaya audios, all transcribed):**
- **Physiological sigh:** two inhales through the nose, one long exhale. 1-2 min before rounds or whenever stressed. This is the best-supported one: a 2023 Stanford study found 5 min a day improved mood more than meditation.
- **Evening:** lying down, in for 2, hold for 2, out for 4 through the nose, for 5 min. Or box breathing in bed.
- **Wim Hof** (3 rounds of about 30 deep breaths, then a breath hold on empty): only lying down, never in water or driving.
- **His 2026 rule:** no tech in the morning until after breathwork or meditation.

**Stress is part of recovery:**
- Mitchell's slides describe how, in his Olympic year, "perfect" sleep, food and recovery still came with recurring injuries and cystic acne. Both cleared once the pressure dropped.
- His lesson: "True healing only happens in rest-and-digest mode."
- Chronic stress raising injury risk is supported by sports research. Treat mental load like training load.

**Sunscreen:**
- His "never wear sunscreen" slides rely on lab studies about infrared light.
- For 4-5 hour rounds, **keep mineral sunscreen (zinc) and a hat.**
- Get your morning and evening light without it.

**Coffee and mornings (from his later videos):**
- Wait about an hour after waking before coffee.
- Stop caffeine by 1-2 PM, because about half is still in you 5 hours later. Your plan already cuts it off at 2 PM.
- Drink salted water or electrolytes *before* coffee.
- Do one thing before touching your phone.

**What you'll do:** your plan already has the morning walk, 9:30 PM screens off, magnesium and glycine. Add:
- A pitch-black, cool room.
- Phone out of the bedroom.
- Dim warm lights after sunset.
- A pinch of salt in your morning water on hot or golf days.

**Decide:** nothing big. These are small tweaks.

## Lesson 9: Alcohol, consistency and what to skip

**The big idea:** Mitchell's biggest single change wasn't a diet. It was cutting alcohol and having a group hold him accountable.

**What they teach:**
- **Mitchell:**
  - "We're going cold turkey without alcohol... my next tournament I did pretty well." He calls it "one of the biggest needle movers."
  - He went animal-based with friends in a group chat where everyone posted their food daily. That's why Kaya is built around challenges.
  - "Forgive the slip." Obsessing backfires.
  - **His best lesson (Ito-san, Japan, Oct 2025):** after a day with a very healthy older man who followed no protocols, Mitchell said: "How much stress am I adding, spending my whole day optimizing my health?... health is a lot more simple than we make it. Balance... don't over-indulge, and enjoy the day."
  - **"One good day" method (after the Olympics):** string one good day onto the next until you have momentum.
  - His own summary: "Only three things really move the needle: caloric deficit, hitting your protein and lifting heavy."

**Skip list (from all four sources):**
- Raw milk and raw meat.
- Sun gazing; multi-day or sardine fasts; 900-kcal cuts.
- Methylene blue, vitamin E megadoses, baking-soda pills, 8-10 g taurine.
- Driving with one leg up on the seat.
- From his 2nd account: ivermectin, fenbendazole and chlorine dioxide (industrial bleach) for colds; raw organs; zeolite "detox"; sponsored NMN.
- 72-80 hour water fasts.
- Vitamin D3 at 10,000-20,000 IU a day ("way too much, but we're not trying to be average"). Your level is 39 on your greens, so retest before changing anything.
- Stem-cell clinics, methylene blue, mouth tape if your nose is blocked.
- "Deuterium" and "no bananas in winter," "fabric frequency" (linen "5,000 Hz"), "structured water," and "never wear sunscreen" for long outdoor days.
- Kip-ups and toe-hooked "fascial Nordics" for now.
- Toe-in walking.
- "Never lift a barbell."
- "Sugar by day, protein at night."

**What you'll do:**
- Set an alcohol rule you'll actually keep. For example: none in the 48 hours before a tournament, and a set weekly cap.
- Pick one accountability method: a friend, a group chat, or checking in with me weekly.

**Decide:** your alcohol rule, and your accountability method.
